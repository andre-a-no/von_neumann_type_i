# 10. Quantum computing

Noise on a quantum computer is a channel, and a channel generates an algebra: the $*$-algebra of
its Kraus operators. Its structure (Chapter 2) tells which information survives the noise untouched;
a condition on products of Kraus operators, involving another algebra, tells which information a
recovery can restore. The last part treats algorithms that conserve a charge: their states live in
the algebra of charge sectors, and the dominant noise moves them between sectors.

**You will learn** why the fixed points of a unital channel form a von Neumann algebra, the
Knill–Laflamme theorem with a complete proof, how to protect only the label of a sector, and how
the XY mixer of the quantum alternating operator ansatz, amplitude damping and post-selection look
in the algebra $\bigoplus_wM_{\binom nw}$ of Hamming-weight sectors.

Throughout, a **channel** on $M_n(\mathbb C)$ is a map $\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^rE_i\rho E_i^*$ with
**Kraus operators** satisfying $\sum_iE_i^*E_i=1$ (trace preserving); it is **unital** if moreover
$\sum_iE_iE_i^*=1$. Its **dual** $\Phi^*(X)=\sum_iE_i^*XE_i$ satisfies
$\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi(\rho)X)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\,\Phi^*(X))$. (Chapter 6, in preparation, proves that every
completely positive map has this form; here channels are *given* by Kraus operators.) The
**interaction algebra** $\mathcal A$ of $\Phi$ is the $*$-algebra generated by $1$ and the $E_i$, the span
of all products of the $E_i$ and $E_i^*$; a matrix commutes with $\mathcal A$ iff it commutes with all
$E_i$ and $E_i^*$. A state $\rho$ is **supported** in a subspace $K$ with projection $P_K$ if
$\rho=P_K\rho P_K$. $X,Y,Z$ are the Pauli matrices, $X_j$ is $X$ on qubit $j$ (numbered $1,\dots,n$;
Section 10.4 follows the library, sites $0,\dots,n-1$), $|b\rangle$, $b\in\{0,1\}^n$, the computational
basis, $|b|$ the **Hamming weight**, and $P_w$ the projection onto $H_w=\operatorname{span}\{|b\rangle:|b|=w\}$.

## 10.1 Noise algebras, noiseless subsystems and fixed points

By Theorem 2.6 there is a unitary $V$ with $V^*\mathcal AV=\bigoplus_cM_{k_c}\otimes1_{m_c}$ and
$V^*\mathcal A'V=\bigoplus_c1_{k_c}\otimes M_{m_c}$: every Kraus operator acts only on the first tensor
factor of each summand.

**Proposition 10.1 (noiseless subsystems; decoherence-free subspaces).** (a) Write
$V^*E_iV=\bigoplus_cE_{i,c}\otimes1_{m_c}$, and let $\rho=V(\sigma\otimes\tau)V^*$, placed in the summand
$c$, for states $\sigma$ on $\mathbb C^{k_c}$ and $\tau$ on $\mathbb C^{m_c}$. Then
$\Phi(\rho)=V(\Phi_c(\sigma)\otimes\tau)V^*$ with the channel $\Phi_c(\sigma)=\sum_iE_{i,c}\sigma E_{i,c}^*$ on
$M_{k_c}$: the state $\tau$ is untouched. Moreover $\Phi^*(X)=X$ for every $X\in\mathcal A'$.
(b) If $K$ is a subspace with $E_iP_K=e_iP_K$, $e_i\in\mathbb C$, for all $i$, then $\Phi(\rho)=\rho$ for
every state $\rho$ supported in $K$.

*Proof.* (a) $\Phi(\rho)=V\sum_i(E_{i,c}\sigma E_{i,c}^*\otimes\tau)V^*$, and $\Phi_c$ is trace preserving
because the summand $c$ of $V^*(\sum_iE_i^*E_i)V=1$ is $(\sum_iE_{i,c}^*E_{i,c})\otimes1_{m_c}$. For
$X\in\mathcal A'$, $\Phi^*(X)=X\sum_iE_i^*E_i=X$. (b) $P_KE_i^*=(E_iP_K)^*=\bar e_iP_K$, so
$E_i\rho E_i^*=E_iP_K\rho P_KE_i^*=|e_i|^2\rho$, and $\sum_i|e_i|^2P_K=P_K\sum_iE_i^*E_iP_K=P_K$. $\square$

A summand with $k_c=1$ is case (b): a **decoherence-free subspace** (Zanardi–Rasetti 1997,
Lidar–Chuang–Whaley 1998). For $k_c>1$ the factor $\mathbb C^{m_c}$ is a **noiseless subsystem**
(Knill–Laflamme–Viola 2000): the noise acts, but only on the other factor. Proposition 10.1 holds
for every channel; for unital channels the preserved observables are *exactly* those of $\mathcal A'$:

**Theorem 10.2 (fixed points of unital channels).** Let $\Phi$ be unital. Then
$$
\operatorname{Fix}(\Phi)=\{X\in M_n(\mathbb C):\Phi(X)=X\}=\mathcal A'=\operatorname{Fix}(\Phi^*).
$$
In particular $\operatorname{Fix}(\Phi)$ is a von Neumann algebra, $*$-isomorphic to $\bigoplus_cM_{m_c}$.

*Proof.* If $X\in\mathcal A'$, then $\Phi(X)=X\sum_iE_iE_i^*=X$. Conversely let $\Phi(X)=X$, so also
$\Phi(X^*)=\Phi(X)^*=X^*$. Expand
$$
\sum_i[E_i,X]^*[E_i,X]=\sum_i\big(X^*E_i^*E_iX-X^*E_i^*XE_i-E_i^*X^*E_iX+E_i^*X^*XE_i\big)
$$
and take traces term by term: the first term gives $\operatorname{Tr}X^*X$ because $\Phi$ is trace preserving;
the second $\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(E_iX^*E_i^*X)=\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi(X^*)X)=\operatorname{Tr}X^*X$; the third
$\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(X^*E_iXE_i^*)=\operatorname{Tr}(X^*\Phi(X))=\operatorname{Tr}X^*X$; the fourth
$\operatorname{Tr}(X^*X\sum_iE_iE_i^*)=\operatorname{Tr}X^*X$ because $\Phi$ is unital. So a sum of positive matrices
$[E_i,X]^*[E_i,X]$ has trace $0$; each has trace $0$, hence vanishes, and $[E_i,X]=0$. The same holds
for $X^*$, so $[E_i^*,X]=-[E_i,X^*]^*=0$ and $X\in\mathcal A'$. Finally $\Phi^*$ is a unital channel with
Kraus operators $E_i^*$ and the same interaction algebra, and Theorem 2.6 gives
$\mathcal A'\cong\bigoplus_cM_{m_c}$. $\square$

Trace preservation enters the first term, unitality the fourth; without unitality the theorem fails
(Exercise 1). (Kribs 2003, Arias–Gheondea–Gudder 2002; infinite dimensions need more hypotheses.)

**Corollary 10.3 (collective dephasing).** Let $U=e^{-i\theta Z_{\mathrm{tot}}}$,
$Z_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_jZ_j$, with $e^{2i\theta d}\neq1$ for $d=1,\dots,n$, and
$\Phi(\rho)=\tfrac12(U\rho U^*+U^*\rho U)$. Then $\mathcal A=\operatorname{span}\{P_0,\dots,P_n\}$, every $H_w$
is a decoherence-free subspace, and $\operatorname{Fix}(\Phi)=\bigoplus_wB(H_w)\cong\bigoplus_wM_{\binom nw}$,
of dimension $\sum_w\binom nw^2$.

*Proof.* $Z_{\mathrm{tot}}|b\rangle=(n-2|b|)|b\rangle$, so $U=\sum_we^{-i\theta(n-2w)}P_w$, with distinct
eigenvalues (their ratios are $e^{2i\theta(w-w')}$). The Lagrange polynomials of the proof of
Theorem 1.5(a) give $P_w=p_w(U)\in\mathcal A$; conversely $U,U^*$ lie in the algebra
$\operatorname{span}\{P_w\}$. So $\mathcal A=\operatorname{span}\{P_w\}$ ($k_c=1$, $m_c=\binom nw$), and Proposition
10.1(b) applies to each $H_w$. $X$ commutes with all $P_w$ iff $P_vXP_{v'}=XP_vP_{v'}=0$ for
$v\neq v'$, i.e. iff $X$ is block diagonal; Theorem 10.2 gives the rest. $\square$

Commutants are null spaces of $X\mapsto(AX-XA)_A$ (Chapter 2), fixed points that of
$\sum_iE_i\otimes\bar E_i-1$ (the matrix of $\Phi-\mathrm{id}$ on row-major vectorised matrices). For $n=4$
the three spaces of the corollary have dimensions $5$, $70=1+16+36+16+1$ and $70$:

```python
import itertools
import math
import torch

torch.manual_seed(0)
dt = torch.complex128
I2, X = torch.eye(2, dtype=dt), torch.tensor([[0, 1], [1, 0]], dtype=dt)
Y, Z = torch.tensor([[0, -1j], [1j, 0]], dtype=dt), torch.tensor([[1, 0], [0, -1]], dtype=dt)

def on(op, j, n):                             # op on qubit j of n (j = 0 is the leftmost factor)
    out = torch.ones(1, 1, dtype=dt)
    for i in range(n):
        out = torch.kron(out, op if i == j else I2)
    return out

def null_space(L, tol=1e-9):                  # orthonormal columns spanning {x : L x = 0}
    w, V = torch.linalg.eigh(L.mH @ L)
    return V[:, w < tol]

def commutant_basis(ops):
    d, ops = ops[0].shape[0], [A.resolve_conj().contiguous() for A in ops]
    eye = torch.eye(d, dtype=dt)
    L = torch.cat([torch.kron(A, eye) - torch.kron(eye, A.T.contiguous()) for A in ops])
    return [v.reshape(d, d) for v in null_space(L).T]

def fixed_points(kraus):                      # vec(E X E^*) = (E (x) conj E) vec(X)
    d = kraus[0].shape[0]
    S = sum(torch.kron(E.resolve_conj(), E.conj().resolve_conj()) for E in kraus)
    return [v.reshape(d, d) for v in null_space(S - torch.eye(d * d, dtype=dt)).T]

n, theta = 4, 0.3
U = torch.linalg.matrix_exp(-1j * theta * sum(on(Z, j, n) for j in range(n)))
kraus = [U / math.sqrt(2), U.mH / math.sqrt(2)]                  # unital: U U^* = U^* U = 1
comm = commutant_basis(kraus + [E.mH for E in kraus])             # A'
A_int, fix = commutant_basis(comm), fixed_points(kraus)           # A = A'' (Theorem 2.2), Fix(Phi)
print(len(A_int), len(comm), len(fix))
assert (len(A_int), len(comm), len(fix)) == (5, 70, 70)
span = torch.linalg.svdvals(torch.stack([F.reshape(-1) for F in comm + fix]))
assert int((span > 1e-9).sum()) == 70                             # Fix(Phi) = A' (Theorem 10.2)
```

$\bigoplus_wM_{\binom nw}$ is the algebra of a `SpinChain` (Chapter 9), whose sectors are the numbers
of up spins, i.e. Hamming weights. There the Kraus operators are central,
$U=\sum_we^{-i\theta(n-2w)}P_w$, and the channel is the identity: the library stores the noiseless part.

```python
from torch_vn_algebra import SpinChain, TypeIAlgebra, DensityMatrix, channels, states, dynamics

M = SpinChain(n).algebra                                          # (+)_w M_binom(4, w)
print(M.k_factors)
assert sum(k * k for k in M.k_factors) == len(fix)
phases = [torch.exp(torch.tensor(-1j * theta * (n - 2 * w), dtype=dt)) for w in range(n + 1)]
Phi_M = channels.kraus_channel([M.central([z / math.sqrt(2) for z in phases]),
                                M.central([z.conj() / math.sqrt(2) for z in phases])])
rho = states.random_density_matrix(M, batch_size=3)
assert (Phi_M(rho).matrix - rho.matrix).abs().max() < 1e-12      # no state of M is disturbed
```

> **Physical meaning.** A fluctuating field that is the same on all qubits shifts the phase of $|b\rangle$
> in proportion to $n-2|b|$. States of one Hamming weight acquire a common phase, so $|01\rangle,|10\rangle$
> encode a qubit immune to this noise (trapped ions: Kielpinski et al., *Science* 291, 2001), while
> coherences between weights decay: the noise imposes a superselection rule (Chapter 3).

**Collective decoherence.** If the field also fluctuates in direction, the Kraus operators are
rotations $e^{-i\theta\,\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S}$, $S_\alpha=\tfrac12\sum_j\sigma_{\alpha,j}$, $\sigma\in\{X,Y,Z\}$,
which lie in the $*$-algebra $\mathcal B$ generated by $1,S_x,S_y,S_z$ (Theorem 1.5(a)).

**Proposition 10.4 (singlet subspace).** Let $K=\ker S_x\cap\ker S_y\cap\ker S_z$. Every channel
whose Kraus operators lie in $\mathcal B$ fixes every state supported in $K$.

*Proof.* $\mathcal B$ is spanned by the words in $S_x,S_y,S_z$ (and $1$); words of length $\ge1$ annihilate
$K$, so each element of $\mathcal B$ acts on $K$ as a scalar. Apply Proposition 10.1(b). $\square$

For $n=4$ the code finds $\dim K=2$, $\dim\mathcal B'=14$, $\dim\mathcal B=35$, as for $\mathcal B\cong M_5\oplus M_3\oplus M_1$
with multiplicities $1,3,2$ (total spin $2,1,0$), which Chapter 4 (in preparation) derives: four
qubits carry a decoherence-free qubit ($k=1$, $m=2$) and a noiseless qutrit ($k=3$, $m=3$).

```python
S = [sum(on(P, j, n) for j in range(n)) / 2 for P in (X, Y, Z)]
comm_S = commutant_basis(S)
print(len(comm_S), len(commutant_basis(comm_S)))
assert len(comm_S) == 14 and len(commutant_basis(comm_S)) == 35
K = null_space(torch.cat(S))                                      # columns: a basis of K
assert K.shape[1] == 2
axes = torch.nn.functional.normalize(torch.randn(3, 3, dtype=torch.float64), dim=1)
rotations = [torch.linalg.matrix_exp(-1j * t * sum(c * Sa for c, Sa in zip(ax, S))) / math.sqrt(3)
             for t, ax in zip(torch.rand(3, dtype=torch.float64) * math.pi, axes)]
apply = lambda kr, r: sum(E @ r @ E.mH for E in kr)
for G in (K @ torch.randn(2, 2, dtype=dt), torch.randn(16, 16, dtype=dt)):  # supported in K / generic
    r = G @ G.mH / (G @ G.mH).trace()
    assert ((apply(rotations, r) - r).abs().max() < 1e-12) == (G.shape[1] == 2)
```

## 10.2 Error correction: the Knill–Laflamme theorem

A **code** is a subspace $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathbb C^n$ with projection $P$. It **corrects** $\Phi$ if
there is a channel $\mathcal R$ (the **recovery**) with $\mathcal R(\Phi(\rho))=\rho$ for every state $\rho$
supported in $\mathcal C$.

**Theorem 10.5 (Knill–Laflamme).** $\mathcal C$ corrects $\Phi$ iff there are numbers $\lambda_{ij}$
with
$$
PE_i^*E_jP=\lambda_{ij}P\qquad\text{for all }i,j.
$$

*Proof.* *Necessity.* Let $\mathcal R$ have Kraus operators $R_m$; then $\mathcal R\circ\Phi$ has the
Kraus operators $R_mE_i$. For a unit vector $\psi\in\mathcal C$,
$\sum_{m,i}R_mE_i|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|E_i^*R_m^*=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$, and for $\phi\perp\psi$ the
expectation in $\phi$ gives $\sum_{m,i}|\langle\phi,R_mE_i\psi\rangle|^2=0$, so $R_mE_i\psi\in\mathbb C\psi$.
So $R_mE_iP=c_{mi}P$: a linear map $T$ with $Tx\in\mathbb Cx$ for all $x$ is a multiple of $1$ (if
$Tx=ax$, $Ty=by$, $T(x+y)=c(x+y)$, $x,y$ independent, then $a=c=b$). As $\sum_mR_m^*R_m=1$,
$PE_i^*E_jP=\sum_m(R_mE_iP)^*(R_mE_jP)=\sum_m\bar c_{mi}c_{mj}P$.

*Sufficiency.* $\Lambda=(\lambda_{ij})$ is Hermitian, as $\lambda_{ji}P=(PE_i^*E_jP)^*=\bar\lambda_{ij}P$, and
positive, as $\sum_{ij}\bar c_ic_j\lambda_{ij}P=PF^*FP\ge0$ for $F=\sum_jc_jE_j$. Write $\Lambda=UDU^*$,
$U$ unitary, $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_k)$, $d_k\ge0$ (Theorem 1.3), and $F_k=\sum_iu_{ik}E_i$. Then
$\sum_kF_k\rho F_k^*=\sum_{ij}(UU^*)_{ij}E_i\rho E_j^*=\Phi(\rho)$ and $PF_k^*F_lP=(U^*\Lambda U)_{kl}P=d_k\delta_{kl}P$.
If $d_k=0$, then $\|F_kPx\|^2=0$ for all $x$, so $F_kP=0$. If $d_k>0$, let $V_k=F_kP/\sqrt{d_k}$; then
$V_k^*V_l=\delta_{kl}P$, so $V_kP=V_k$, the $Q_k=V_kV_k^*$ are mutually orthogonal projections and
$Q=\sum_kQ_k$ is a projection. The recovery
$$
\mathcal R(\sigma)=\sum_{k:d_k>0}V_k^*\sigma V_k+(1-Q)\sigma(1-Q)
$$
is a channel, since $\sum_kV_kV_k^*+(1-Q)^2=1$. For $\rho=P\rho P$, $\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{d_k>0}d_kV_k\rho V_k^*$;
since $V_l^*V_k=\delta_{kl}P$ and $(1-Q)V_k=(1-Q)Q_kV_k=0$, $\mathcal R(\Phi(\rho))=\sum_kd_k\rho$, and
$\sum_kd_k=\operatorname{Tr}\Lambda=1$ because $\sum_i\lambda_{ii}P=P\sum_iE_i^*E_iP=P$. $\square$

(Knill–Laflamme 1997; Nielsen–Chuang, Theorem 10.1.) $\mathcal R$ measures the **syndrome** — which of
the orthogonal subspaces $Q_k\mathbb C^n$ contains the state — and rotates the state back into the code.

**Example: the bit-flip code.** $\mathcal C=\operatorname{span}\{|000\rangle,|111\rangle\}$ and "at most one bit
flip", $E_0=\sqrt{p_0}\,1$, $E_j=\sqrt{p_j}\,X_j$. For $i\neq j$, $X_iX_j$ (with $X_0=1$) flips one or two
bits and maps $\mathcal C$ into $\mathcal C^\perp$; so $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(p_0,\dots,p_3)$, $V_k=X_kP$,
$Q=1$ and $\mathcal R(\sigma)=\sum_{k=0}^3PX_k\sigma X_kP$. If every qubit flips independently with
probability $p$, the condition fails, and $\mathcal R$ maps $|0_L\rangle=|000\rangle$ to $|1_L\rangle$ whenever
$\ge2$ flips occur: the fidelity is $1-3p^2+2p^3=0.972$ for $p=0.1$. In the library $M_8$ is an
algebra with one block, and `R @ Phi` is the composition $\mathcal R\circ\Phi$:

```python
M8 = TypeIAlgebra([8], [8], precision='double')
op8 = lambda T: M8.operator(T.reshape(1, 1, 8, 8))
state8 = lambda v: DensityMatrix(M8, matrix=torch.outer(v, v.conj()).reshape(1, 1, 8, 8))
ket = lambda bits: torch.eye(8, dtype=dt)[int(bits, 2)]
def kl_matrix(kraus, P, tol=1e-12):
    """(lambda_ij) with P E_i^* E_j P = lambda_ij P, or None if the Knill-Laflamme condition fails."""
    lam = torch.tensor([[(P @ a.mH @ b @ P).trace() / P.trace() for b in kraus] for a in kraus])
    ok = all((P @ a.mH @ b @ P - lam[i, j] * P).abs().max() < tol
             for i, a in enumerate(kraus) for j, b in enumerate(kraus))
    return lam if ok else None
def independent(op, prob):                    # op on each qubit independently with probability prob
    return [math.sqrt(prob ** sum(s) * (1 - prob) ** (3 - sum(s)))
            * torch.linalg.multi_dot([on(op, j, 3) if s[j] else torch.eye(8, dtype=dt) for j in range(3)])
            for s in itertools.product([0, 1], repeat=3)]

P = torch.outer(ket('000'), ket('000')) + torch.outer(ket('111'), ket('111'))
flips = [torch.eye(8, dtype=dt)] + [on(X, j, 3) for j in range(3)]
E_one = [math.sqrt(pk) * Xk for pk, Xk in zip((0.7, 0.1, 0.1, 0.1), flips)]
assert torch.allclose(kl_matrix(E_one, P), torch.diag(torch.tensor([0.7, 0.1, 0.1, 0.1], dtype=dt)))
Phi_one = channels.kraus_channel([op8(E) for E in E_one])
R = channels.kraus_channel([op8(P @ Xk) for Xk in flips])        # syndrome measurement + correction
assert R.is_trace_preserving()
a, b = torch.nn.functional.normalize(torch.randn(2, dtype=dt), dim=0)
rho_L = state8(a * ket('000') + b * ket('111'))
assert abs((R @ Phi_one)(rho_L).fidelity(rho_L).item() - 1) < 1e-12       # Theorem 10.5
p = 0.1                                                           # independent flips: KL fails
E_ind = independent(X, p)
assert kl_matrix(E_ind, P) is None
zero_L = state8(ket('000'))
F = (R @ channels.kraus_channel([op8(E) for E in E_ind]))(zero_L).fidelity(zero_L).item()
print(f"fidelity after recovery {F:.4f}, exact {1 - 3 * p**2 + 2 * p**3:.4f}")
assert abs(F - (1 - 3 * p**2 + 2 * p**3)) < 1e-12
```

## 10.3 Operator algebra error correction

The Heisenberg picture allows one to protect less than the whole code. Let $\mathfrak A$ be a von
Neumann algebra on $\mathcal C=P\mathbb C^n$ (a $*$-algebra of operators on $\mathcal C$ containing $P$),
the observables to be protected. $\mathfrak A$ is **correctable** for $\Phi$ if there is a channel
$\mathcal R$ with
$$
P\,(\mathcal R\circ\Phi)^*(A)\,P=A\qquad\text{for all }A\in\mathfrak A,
$$
i.e. $\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal R(\Phi(\rho))A)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho A)$ for all states $\rho$ supported in $\mathcal C$
and $A\in\mathfrak A$. For $\mathfrak A=B(\mathcal C)$ this is correction as in Section 10.2: if
$\sigma=\mathcal R(\Phi(\rho))$ has $P\sigma P=\rho$, then $\operatorname{Tr}(1-P)\sigma(1-P)=0$, so $\sigma^{1/2}(1-P)=0$ and
$\sigma=\rho$. The opposite extreme is a commutative algebra: only a classical label is protected.

**Proposition 10.6 (correcting a sector label).** Let $P=\sum_{c=1}^CP_c$ with mutually orthogonal
projections $P_c\neq0$, and $\mathfrak A=\operatorname{span}\{P_1,\dots,P_C\}$. Equivalent are:
1. there are $N_1,\dots,N_C\ge0$, $\sum_cN_c=1$, with $\operatorname{Tr}(N_c\Phi(\rho))=1$ for every $c$ and
   every state $\rho$ supported in $P_c\mathbb C^n$;
2. $P_cE_i^*E_jP_d=0$ for all $i,j$ and $c\neq d$, i.e. $PE_i^*E_jP\in\mathfrak A'$, the commutant of
   $\mathfrak A$ in $B(\mathcal C)$;
3. $\mathfrak A$ is correctable for $\Phi$.

*Proof.* $T\in B(\mathcal C)$ commutes with all $P_c$ iff $P_cTP_d=0$ for $c\neq d$ (as in Corollary 10.3).

(2)$\Rightarrow$(1). The subspaces $S_c=\operatorname{span}\{E_iP_cx\}$ are mutually orthogonal, as
$\langle E_iP_cx,E_jP_dy\rangle=\langle x,P_cE_i^*E_jP_dy\rangle=0$. Let $N_c$ be the projection onto $S_c$ for
$c<C$ and $N_C=1-\sum_{c<C}N_c$, also a projection. For $\rho$ supported in $P_c\mathbb C^n$,
$\Phi(\rho)=\sum_iE_iP_c\rho P_cE_i^*$ has range in $S_c$, so $\operatorname{Tr}(N_c\Phi(\rho))=1$.
(1)$\Rightarrow$(2). For a unit vector $x\in P_c\mathbb C^n$ and $d\neq c$,
$0=\operatorname{Tr}(N_d\Phi(|x\rangle\langle x|))=\sum_i\|N_d^{1/2}E_ix\|^2$; so $N_dE_iP_c=0$ and, summing over
$d$, $N_cE_iP_c=E_iP_c$. Hence $P_cE_i^*E_jP_d=P_cE_i^*N_cE_jP_d=0$ for $c\neq d$.
(1),(2)$\Rightarrow$(3). Let $\omega_c=P_c/\operatorname{Tr}P_c$ and $\mathcal R(\sigma)=\sum_c\operatorname{Tr}(N_c\sigma)\,\omega_c$
("measure, then prepare"), a channel with the Kraus operators $\sqrt\mu\,|u\rangle\langle f|N_c^{1/2}$ for the
eigenpairs $(\mu,u)$ of $\omega_c$ and the vectors $f$ of an orthonormal basis. Since
$\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal R(\sigma)P_d)=\operatorname{Tr}(N_d\sigma)$, $\mathcal R^*(P_d)=N_d$. Using $N_dE_iP_c=\delta_{cd}E_iP_c$
(from the previous step) and then 2,
$$
P(\mathcal R\circ\Phi)^*(P_d)P=P\,\Phi^*(N_d)\,P=\sum_i\sum_{c,c'}P_{c'}E_i^*N_dE_iP_c=\sum_i\sum_{c'}P_{c'}E_i^*E_iP_d=P_d\Big(\sum_iE_i^*E_i\Big)P_d=P_d .
$$
(3)$\Rightarrow$(1). Let $N_c=\mathcal R^*(P_c)$ for $c<C$ and $N_C=\mathcal R^*(1-\sum_{c<C}P_c)$; they are
positive and sum to $\mathcal R^*(1)=1$. For $\rho$ supported in $P_c\mathbb C^n$ and $d<C$,
$\operatorname{Tr}(N_d\Phi(\rho))=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\,P(\mathcal R\circ\Phi)^*(P_d)P)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho P_d)=\delta_{cd}$, hence also
$\operatorname{Tr}(N_C\Phi(\rho))=\delta_{cC}$. $\square$

**Theorem 10.7 (Bény–Kempf–Kribs 2007; stated without proof).** A von Neumann algebra $\mathfrak A$
on $\mathcal C$ is correctable for $\Phi$ iff $PE_i^*E_jP\in\mathfrak A'$ for all $i,j$, where
$\mathfrak A'$ is the commutant of $\mathfrak A$ in $B(\mathcal C)$.

For $\mathfrak A=B(\mathcal C)$, $\mathfrak A'=\mathbb CP$ and this is Theorem 10.5; Proposition 10.6 is the
case of a commutative $\mathfrak A$. For
$\mathfrak A=M_k\otimes1_m$ on $\mathcal C=\mathbb C^k\otimes\mathbb C^m$ one obtains the operator (subsystem)
codes of Kribs–Laflamme–Poulin (*Phys. Rev. Lett.* 94, 180501, 2005), which contain the noiseless
subsystems of Section 10.1 with $\mathcal R=\mathrm{id}$. For the proof see C. Bény, A. Kempf, D. W.
Kribs, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 98, 100502 (2007) and *Phys. Rev. A* 76, 042303 (2007).

**Example.** The repetition code under independent phase flips (probability $q$ per qubit) followed
by at most one bit flip. The Kraus operators are multiples of $X_kZ^s$, $s\in\{0,1\}^3$, and each
$E_i^*E_j$ flips at most two bits, so condition 2 holds for $P_0=|000\rangle\langle000|$,
$P_1=|111\rangle\langle111|$: the classical bit survives, read out by majority vote,
$N_0=\sum_{|b|\le1}|b\rangle\langle b|$, $N_1=1-N_0$. The qubit does not: $PZ_1P$ is not a multiple of $P$,
and after $\mathcal R$ the state $|{+_L}\rangle=(|000\rangle+|111\rangle)/\sqrt2$ suffers the logical phase flip
$Z_1Z_2Z_3$ exactly when an odd number of phase flips occurred; its fidelity is
$(1+(1-2q)^3)/2=0.8645$ for $q=0.05$.

```python
q = 0.05
Phi2 = Phi_one @ channels.kraus_channel([op8(E) for E in independent(Z, q)])   # dephasing, then a flip
E2 = [Phi2.kraus[0, i, 0] for i in range(Phi2.kraus_rank)]        # its 32 Kraus operators
P0, P1 = torch.outer(ket('000'), ket('000')), torch.outer(ket('111'), ket('111'))
assert kl_matrix(E2, P) is None                                   # the qubit is not protected
assert max((P0 @ e.mH @ f @ P1).abs().max().item() for e in E2 for f in E2) == 0      # condition 2
N0 = torch.diag(torch.tensor([float(bin(i).count('1') <= 1) for i in range(8)], dtype=dt))
for N, Pd in ((N0, P0), (torch.eye(8, dtype=dt) - N0, P1)):       # P Phi^*(N_d) P = P_d
    assert (P @ sum(e.mH @ N @ e for e in E2) @ P - Pd).abs().max() < 1e-12
votes = states.born_probabilities(Phi2(rho_L), [op8(N0), op8(torch.eye(8, dtype=dt) - N0)])[0]
assert torch.allclose(votes, torch.stack([abs(a) ** 2, abs(b) ** 2]))      # the classical bit survives
plus_L = state8((ket('000') + ket('111')) / math.sqrt(2))
F_plus = (R @ Phi2)(plus_L).fidelity(plus_L).item()
print(f"fidelity of |+_L> {F_plus:.4f}, exact {(1 + (1 - 2 * q)**3) / 2:.4f}")
assert abs(F_plus - (1 + (1 - 2 * q) ** 3) / 2) < 1e-12
```

> **Physical meaning.** Protecting a commutative algebra means protecting classical information (a
> bit, the label of a superselection sector), which is much cheaper than protecting a qubit: noise
> commuting with the sector projections is harmless. "Hybrid codes" correspond to $\bigoplus_cM_{k_c}$.

## 10.4 Constrained mixers

The quantum alternating operator ansatz (Hadfield et al., *Algorithms* 12, 34, 2019) prepares
$|\beta,\gamma\rangle=e^{-i\beta_pH_M}e^{-i\gamma_pH_C}\cdots e^{-i\beta_1H_M}e^{-i\gamma_1H_C}|b_0\rangle$, $H_C$ a diagonal
cost Hamiltonian, $H_M$ a **mixer**. For a constraint $|b|=w$ (graph bisection, portfolios of $w$
assets) one takes $|b_0|=w$ and the **XY mixer** on a ring, $H_M=\tfrac14\sum_{(i,j)}(X_iX_j+Y_iY_j)$.

**Proposition 10.8.** $H_M$, $H_C$, $e^{-i\beta H_M}$ and $e^{-i\gamma H_C}$ lie in
$\mathcal M=\{P_0,\dots,P_n\}'=\bigoplus_wB(H_w)\cong\bigoplus_wM_{\binom nw}$, so $|\beta,\gamma\rangle\in H_w$:
the ansatz never leaves the feasible subspace.

*Proof.* On qubits $i,j$, $X\otimes X+Y\otimes Y=2(|01\rangle\langle10|+|10\rangle\langle01|)$ (use $Y|0\rangle=i|1\rangle$,
$Y|1\rangle=-i|0\rangle$): $X_iX_j+Y_iY_j$ swaps the bits $i,j$ when they differ and annihilates $|b\rangle$
otherwise, so it maps each $H_w$ into itself. A diagonal $H_C$ commutes with the diagonal $P_w$, and
so do the exponentials (Theorem 1.5(d)). $\square$

In the library $\mathcal M$ is `SpinChain(n).algebra`, and $H_M$ is the XXZ chain with $J=1$,
$\Delta=0$, as $S^+_iS^-_j+S^-_iS^+_j=\tfrac12(X_iX_j+Y_iY_j)$; we check it against the dense
$64\times64$ matrix. For $H_C$ we take graph bisection on the triangles $\{0,1,2\}$, $\{3,4,5\}$ joined
by the edges $(2,3)$, $(5,0)$: $H_C=\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\tfrac12(1-Z_iZ_j)$, $Z_i=2S^z_i$, counts the cut
edges. The best bisection cuts $2$ edges; we start from $|101010\rangle$, which cuts $6$.

```python
n, w = 6, 3
chain = SpinChain(n, 'periodic')
M = chain.algebra                                                 # (+)_w M_binom(6, w), padded to 20
H_M = chain.xxz(J=1.0, Delta=0.0)
H_dense = sum(on(X, i, n) @ on(X, j, n) + on(Y, i, n) @ on(Y, j, n) for i, j in chain.bonds) / 4
spec = torch.sort(torch.cat([e[0] for e in H_M.eigenvalues()]))[0]
assert torch.allclose(spec, torch.linalg.eigvalsh(H_dense))       # same spectrum, sector by sector
H_C = M.zero()
for i, j in chain.bonds + [(0, 2), (3, 5)]:
    H_C = H_C + (M.identity() - chain.sz(i) @ chain.sz(j) * 4.0) * 0.5
layers = [dynamics.propagator(H_M, beta) @ dynamics.propagator(H_C, gamma)
          for gamma, beta in ((0.6, 1.0), (1.4, 1.4))]
rho0 = ideal = chain.basis_state('101010')
for U_l in layers:
    ideal = channels.unitary_channel(U_l)(ideal)
assert abs(ideal.sector_probabilities()[0, w].item() - 1) < 1e-12           # Proposition 10.8
```

**Amplitude damping** of qubit $i$ ($|1\rangle\to|0\rangle$ with probability $g$) has the Kraus operators
$K_0^{(i)}=|0\rangle\langle0|_i+\sqrt{1-g}\,|1\rangle\langle1|_i$ and $K_1^{(i)}=\sqrt g\,|0\rangle\langle1|_i$, which lowers
the weight: a map between sectors, `chain.site_amplitude_damping(i, g)` (an `InterSectorChannel`).

**Proposition 10.9 (leakage and post-selection).** Let $\rho_0$ be supported in $H_w$, $U_l$
unitaries in $\mathcal M$, $\mathcal D$ the damping of every qubit, and $\rho_l=\mathcal D(U_l\rho_{l-1}U_l^*)$.
Then every $\rho_l$ is block diagonal with weights $\le w$, and
$$
P_w\rho_pP_w=(1-g)^{wp}\;U\rho_0U^*,\qquad U=U_p\cdots U_1 :
$$
the weight $w$ is found with probability $(1-g)^{wp}$, and the post-selected state is the noiseless one.

*Proof.* The Kraus operators of $\mathcal D$ are $K_s=\prod_iK^{(i)}_{s_i}$, $s\in\{0,1\}^n$; $K_s$ maps
$H_v$ into $H_{v-|s|}$, i.e. $P_vK_s=K_sP_{v+|s|}$ ($P_v=0$ for $v>n$). If $\sigma$ is block diagonal with
weights $\le w$, then $P_vK_s\sigma K_s^*P_{v'}=K_sP_{v+|s|}\sigma P_{v'+|s|}K_s^*$ vanishes for $v\neq v'$ and
for $v>w-|s|$: $\mathcal D(\sigma)$ is block diagonal with weights $\le w$, and its weight-$w$ block comes
from $s=0$ alone. As $K_0|b\rangle=(1-g)^{|b|/2}|b\rangle$, $P_w\mathcal D(\sigma)P_w=(1-g)^wP_w\sigma P_w$. The $U_l$
commute with the $P_v$; induct on $l$. $\square$

An `InterSectorChannel` keeps only the diagonal blocks of its output; here there are no others, so
nothing is lost. With $g=0.05$ and $p=2$ the acceptance is $0.95^6=0.7351$:

```python
g = 0.05
damping = [chain.site_amplitude_damping(i, g) for i in range(n)]
noisy = rho0
for U_l in layers:
    noisy = channels.unitary_channel(U_l)(noisy)
    for D in damping:                                             # one qubit after the other
        noisy = D(noisy)
print(noisy.sector_probabilities()[0])                            # weight leaks to w = 2, 1, 0
P_w = M.central([1.0 if v == w else 0.0 for v in range(n + 1)])
post, accept = noisy.condition_on(P_w)                            # measure the weight, keep w = 3
cut = lambda r: states.expectation(r, H_C).real.item()
print(f"P(weight 3) {accept.item():.4f}, fidelity {noisy.fidelity(ideal).item():.4f}, "
      f"post-selected {post.fidelity(ideal).item():.4f}; "
      f"cut: ideal {cut(ideal):.4f}, noisy {cut(noisy):.4f}, post-selected {cut(post):.4f}")
assert abs(accept.item() - (1 - g) ** (w * len(layers))) < 1e-12              # Proposition 10.9
assert abs(post.fidelity(ideal).item() - 1) < 1e-10 and abs(cut(post) - cut(ideal)) < 1e-10
```

The infeasible part lowers the expected cut from $4.3789$ to $4.3123$, a spurious improvement that
would mislead an optimiser of the angles; after post-selection the value is exact.

**Continuous time.** The same holds for the Lindblad equation (Chapter 8)
$$
\dot\rho=-i[H,\rho]+\gamma_1\sum_i\Big(S^-_i\rho S^+_i-\tfrac12\{S^+_iS^-_i,\rho\}\Big)+\gamma_2\sum_k\Big(L_k\rho L_k^*-\tfrac12\{L_k^*L_k,\rho\}\Big)
$$
with $H\in\mathcal M$ and jumps $L_k\in\mathcal M$ inside the sectors, e.g. pure dephasing $L_k=S^z_k$.
Here $\sum_iS^+_iS^-_i=\sum_i|1\rangle\langle1|_i=N$, the **number operator**, $NP_v=vP_v$: it is central.

**Proposition 10.10.** If $\rho(0)$ is supported in $H_w$, then $P_w\rho(t)P_w=e^{-\gamma_1wt}\sigma(t)$,
where $\sigma$ solves the same equation with $\gamma_1=0$ and $\sigma(0)=\rho(0)$. Hence the acceptance is
$e^{-\gamma_1wt}$, and the post-selected state $\sigma(t)$ does not depend on $\gamma_1$.

*Proof.* Let $B_{vv'}=P_v\rho P_{v'}$. As $H,L_k,N$ commute with the $P_v$ and $P_vS^-_i=S^-_iP_{v+1}$,
$\dot B_{vv'}=-i[H,B_{vv'}]+\gamma_1\sum_iS^-_iB_{v+1,v'+1}S^+_i-\tfrac{\gamma_1}2(v+v')B_{vv'}+\gamma_2\mathcal L_2(B_{vv'})$,
$\mathcal L_2$ the dephasing part. The blocks with $\max(v,v')>w$ satisfy a closed linear system with
zero initial values, so they vanish (the solution of $\dot x=Gx$ is $e^{tG}x(0)$, since
$\frac d{dt}(e^{-tG}x)=0$). Then $\dot B_{ww}=-i[H,B_{ww}]-\gamma_1wB_{ww}+\gamma_2\mathcal L_2(B_{ww})$, so
$e^{\gamma_1wt}B_{ww}$ solves the equation of $\sigma$ with the same initial value, and equals $\sigma$.
That generator has trace zero (cyclicity), so $\operatorname{Tr}\sigma(t)=1$. $\square$

For $\gamma_2=0$, $\sigma(t)=e^{-iHt}\rho(0)e^{iHt}$: post-selection is perfect. The companion paper (`examples/paper/ex9_qaoa_xy_mixer.py`) evolves
$|111000\rangle$ under $H_M$ for $T=2$; we reproduce its row $\gamma_1=\gamma_2=0.1$: $P(w=3)=e^{-0.6}=0.5488$,
fidelity $0.4805$, after post-selection $0.8755$, as for $\gamma_1=0$.

```python
T = 2.0
jumps = [chain.lowering(i) for i in range(n)] + [chain.sz(i) for i in range(n)]
rho0 = chain.basis_state('111000')                                # the initial state of the paper
ideal_T = dynamics.von_neumann(rho0, H_M, [T])[0]
post_T = {}
for g1, g2 in ((0.1, 0.0), (0.0, 0.1), (0.1, 0.1)):          # the last one is printed
    rho_T = dynamics.lindblad_evolve(rho0, H_M, jumps, [0.0, T], rates=[g1] * n + [g2] * n, substeps=80)[-1]
    post_T[g1, g2], accept_T = rho_T.condition_on(P_w)
    assert abs(accept_T.item() - math.exp(-w * g1 * T)) < 1e-8                 # Proposition 10.10
print(f"P(weight 3) {accept_T.item():.4f}, fidelity {rho_T.fidelity(ideal_T).item():.4f}, "
      f"post-selected {post_T[0.1, 0.1].fidelity(ideal_T).item():.4f}")
assert abs(post_T[0.1, 0.0].fidelity(ideal_T).item() - 1) < 1e-6               # gamma_2 = 0: perfect
assert post_T[0.1, 0.1].trace_distance(post_T[0.0, 0.1]).item() < 1e-6       # independent of gamma_1
```

> **Physical meaning.** Energy relaxation ($T_1$), a leading error of superconducting qubits, is a
> jump to a lower sector. Measuring the Hamming weight — a central projection, harmless inside a
> sector (Section 3.3) — detects it, and post-selection removes it at the price of the acceptance
> rate. Dephasing commutes with the sector projections and is not detected.

## 10.5 What to compare with

Qiskit (`qiskit.quantum_info`, Aer noise models, `QAOAAnsatz` with a custom mixer), Cirq, PennyLane
(`qml.qaoa.xy_mixer`, `default.mixed`) and QuTiP/`qutip-qip` simulate all of this on $n$ full qubits
and are the right tools for circuits; stim samples Clifford circuits with Pauli noise on thousands of
qubits, but not amplitude damping. None computes interaction
algebras, commutants or fixed points; for numerical block decompositions of matrix $*$-algebras see
Murota et al., *Japan J. Indust. Appl. Math.* 27 (2010). The library adds storage in
$\bigoplus_wM_{\binom nw}$, maps between sectors and post-selection as a central projection, batched.
One sector needs $\binom nw^2$ entries instead of $4^n$ ($1.7\cdot10^8$ versus $4.3\cdot10^9$ for $n=16$,
$w=8$), but the full algebra is padded to $(n+1)\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}^2$ entries ($2.8\cdot10^9$), a
modest saving. It is not a circuit simulator and has no stabilizer formalism.
