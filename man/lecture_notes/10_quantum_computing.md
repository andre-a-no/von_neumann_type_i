# 10. Quantum computing

Noise on a quantum computer is a channel, and a channel generates an algebra: the $`*`$-algebra of
its Kraus operators. Its structure (Chapter 2) tells which information survives the noise untouched;
a condition on products of Kraus operators, involving another algebra, tells which information a
recovery can restore. The last part treats algorithms that conserve a charge: their states live in
the algebra of charge sectors, and amplitude damping moves them between sectors.

**You will learn** why a unital channel and its dual have the same fixed points (the commutant of the noise), the
Knill–Laflamme theorem with a complete proof, how to protect only the label of a sector, and how
the XY mixer of the quantum alternating operator ansatz, amplitude damping and post-selection look
in the algebra $`\bigoplus_wM_{\binom nw}`$ of Hamming-weight sectors. The numerical checks of this chapter are in
[Tutorial 10](../tutorial/10_quantum_computing.md).

Throughout, a **channel** on $`M_D(\mathbb C)`$ is a map $`\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^rE_i\rho E_i^*`$ with
**Kraus operators** satisfying $`\sum_iE_i^*E_i=1`$ (trace preserving); it is **unital** if moreover
$`\sum_iE_iE_i^*=1`$. Its **dual** $`\Phi^*(T)=\sum_iE_i^*TE_i`$ satisfies
$`\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi(\rho)T)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\Phi^*(T))`$ (Section 6.2). (By Theorem 6.2 every completely
positive map has this form; here channels are *given* by Kraus operators.) The
**interaction algebra** $`\mathcal A`$ of $`\Phi`$ is the $`*`$-algebra generated by $`1`$ and the $`E_i`$, the span
of all products of the $`E_i`$ and $`E_i^*`$; a matrix commutes with $`\mathcal A`$ iff it commutes with all
$`E_i`$ and $`E_i^*`$. A state $`\rho`$ is **supported** in a subspace $`K`$ with projection $`P_K`$ if
$`\rho=P_K\rho P_K`$. On $`n`$ qubits, $`D=2^n`$; $`X,Y,Z`$ are the Pauli matrices, $`Z=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)`$, $`X_j`$ is $`X`$ on qubit $`j`$ (numbered $`1,\dots,n`$;
Section 10.4 follows the library, sites $`0,\dots,n-1`$), $`|b\rangle`$, $`b\in\{0,1\}^n`$, the computational
basis, $`|b|`$ the **Hamming weight**, and $`P_w`$ the projection onto $`H_w=\mathrm{span}\{|b\rangle:|b|=w\}`$.

## 10.1 Noise algebras, noiseless subsystems and fixed points

By Theorem 2.6 there is a unitary $`V`$ with $`V^*\mathcal AV=\bigoplus_cM_{k_c}\otimes1_{m_c}`$ and
$`V^*\mathcal A'V=\bigoplus_c1_{k_c}\otimes M_{m_c}`$: every Kraus operator acts only on the first tensor
factor of each summand.

<a id="r-10-1"></a>

**Proposition 10.1 (noiseless subsystems; decoherence-free subspaces).** (a) Write
$`V^*E_iV=\bigoplus_cE_{i,c}\otimes1_{m_c}`$, and let $`\rho=V(\sigma\otimes\tau)V^*`$, placed in the summand
$`c`$, for states $`\sigma`$ on $`\mathbb C^{k_c}`$ and $`\tau`$ on $`\mathbb C^{m_c}`$. Then
$`\Phi(\rho)=V(\Phi_c(\sigma)\otimes\tau)V^*`$ with the channel $`\Phi_c(\sigma)=\sum_iE_{i,c}\sigma E_{i,c}^*`$ on
$`M_{k_c}`$: the state $`\tau`$ is untouched. Moreover $`\Phi^*(T)=T`$ for every $`T\in\mathcal A'`$.
(b) If $`K`$ is a subspace with $`E_iP_K=e_iP_K`$, $`e_i\in\mathbb C`$, for all $`i`$, then $`\Phi(\rho)=\rho`$ for
every state $`\rho`$ supported in $`K`$.

*Proof.* (a) $`\Phi(\rho)=V\sum_i(E_{i,c}\sigma E_{i,c}^*\otimes\tau)V^*`$, and $`\Phi_c`$ is trace preserving
because the summand $`c`$ of $`V^*(\sum_iE_i^*E_i)V=1`$ is $`(\sum_iE_{i,c}^*E_{i,c})\otimes1_{m_c}`$. For
$`T\in\mathcal A'`$, $`\Phi^*(T)=T\sum_iE_i^*E_i=T`$. (b) $`P_KE_i^*=(E_iP_K)^*=\bar e_iP_K`$, so
$`E_i\rho E_i^*=E_iP_K\rho P_KE_i^*=|e_i|^2\rho`$, and $`\sum_i|e_i|^2P_K=P_K\sum_iE_i^*E_iP_K=P_K`$. $`\square`$

A summand with $`k_c=1`$ is case (b): a **decoherence-free subspace** (Zanardi–Rasetti 1997,
Lidar–Chuang–Whaley 1998). For $`k_c>1`$ the factor $`\mathbb C^{m_c}`$ is a **noiseless subsystem**
(Knill–Laflamme–Viola 2000): the noise acts, but only on the other factor. Proposition 10.1 holds
for every channel; for unital channels the preserved observables are *exactly* those of $`\mathcal A'`$:

<a id="r-10-2"></a>

**Theorem 10.2 (fixed points of unital channels).** Let $`\Phi`$ be unital. Then
```math
\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi)=\{T\in M_D(\mathbb C):\Phi(T)=T\}=\mathcal A'=\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi^*).
```
In particular $`\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi)`$ is a von Neumann algebra, $`*`$-isomorphic to $`\bigoplus_cM_{m_c}`$.

*Proof.* The first equality is Theorem 6.10 for the algebra $`M_D(\mathbb C)`$ (one block): $`T`$ is a fixed
point iff it commutes with all $`E_i`$ and $`E_i^*`$, i.e. iff $`T\in\mathcal A'`$. The dual $`\Phi^*`$ is again a
unital channel: its Kraus operators $`E_i^*`$ satisfy $`\sum_iE_iE_i^*=1`$ (trace preserving) and
$`\sum_iE_i^*E_i=1`$ (unital), and they generate the same $`*`$-algebra $`\mathcal A`$. So Theorem 6.10 applied
to $`\Phi^*`$ gives $`\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi^*)=\mathcal A'`$. The last claim is Theorem 2.6. $`\square`$

*Computation:* $`\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi)=\mathcal A'`$ for collective dephasing, with commutants and fixed points computed as null spaces — [tutorial, Section 10.1](../tutorial/10_quantum_computing.md#s-10-1).

For every channel $`\mathcal A'\subseteq\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi^*)`$ (Proposition 10.1(a)), but without
unitality the theorem fails: for amplitude damping $`\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi)=\mathbb C|0\rangle\langle0|`$, whereas
$`\mathcal A'=\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi^*)=\mathbb C1`$ (Section 6.4 and Exercise 1). (Historically: Kribs 2003,
Arias–Gheondea–Gudder 2002.)

<a id="r-10-3"></a>

**Corollary 10.3 (collective dephasing).** Let $`U=e^{-i\theta Z_{\mathrm{tot}}}`$,
$`Z_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_jZ_j`$, with $`e^{2i\theta d}\neq1`$ for $`d=1,\dots,n`$, and
$`\Phi(\rho)=\tfrac12(U\rho U^*+U^*\rho U)`$. Then $`\mathcal A=\mathrm{span}\{P_0,\dots,P_n\}`$, every $`H_w`$
is a decoherence-free subspace, and $`\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi)=\bigoplus_wB(H_w)\cong\bigoplus_wM_{\binom nw}`$,
of dimension $`\sum_w\binom nw^2`$.

*Proof.* $`Z_{\mathrm{tot}}|b\rangle=(n-2|b|)|b\rangle`$, so $`U=\sum_we^{-i\theta(n-2w)}P_w`$, with distinct
eigenvalues (their ratios are $`e^{2i\theta(w-w')}`$, $`0<|w-w'|\le n`$). The Lagrange polynomials of the proof of
Theorem 1.5(a) give $`P_w=p_w(U)\in\mathcal A`$; conversely $`U,U^*`$ lie in the algebra
$`\mathrm{span}\{P_w\}`$. So $`\mathcal A=\mathrm{span}\{P_w\}`$ ($`k_c=1`$, $`m_c=\binom nw`$), and Proposition
10.1(b) applies to each $`H_w`$. If $`T`$ commutes with all $`P_w`$, then $`P_vTP_{v'}=TP_vP_{v'}=0`$ for
$`v\neq v'`$, i.e. $`T`$ is block diagonal, and conversely; Theorem 10.2 gives the rest. $`\square`$

*Computation:* the dimensions $`5`$, $`70`$, $`70`$ for $`n=4`$ — [tutorial, Section 10.1](../tutorial/10_quantum_computing.md#s-10-1). *In the library:* $`\bigoplus_wM_{\binom nw}`$ is `SpinChain(n).algebra`, on which this channel is the identity — [tutorial, Section 10.1](../tutorial/10_quantum_computing.md#s-10-1).

> **Physical meaning** (physical interpretation, not a theorem of this course). A fluctuating field that is the same on all qubits shifts the phase of $`|b\rangle`$
> in proportion to $`n-2|b|`$. States of one Hamming weight acquire a common phase, so $`|01\rangle,|10\rangle`$
> encode a qubit immune to this noise (trapped ions: Kielpinski et al., *Science* 291, 2001), while
> coherences between weights decay: the noise imposes a superselection rule (Section 3.3).

**Collective decoherence.** We call a channel **collective** if its Kraus operators are multiples of
rotations $`e^{-i\theta\,\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S}`$ ($`\theta\in\mathbb R`$, $`\mathbf n\in\mathbb R^3`$ a unit vector),
$`S_\alpha=\tfrac12\sum_j\sigma_{\alpha,j}`$, $`\sigma\in\{X,Y,Z\}`$; this models a field that also fluctuates in direction
(the model, not a theorem). As $`\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S`$ is self-adjoint, $`e^{-i\theta\,\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S}`$ is a
polynomial in it (proof of Theorem 1.5(a)), so these Kraus operators lie in the $`*`$-algebra $`\mathcal B`$ generated by
$`1,S_x,S_y,S_z`$.

<a id="r-10-4"></a>

**Proposition 10.4 (singlet subspace).** Let $`K=\ker S_x\cap\ker S_y\cap\ker S_z`$. Every channel
whose Kraus operators lie in $`\mathcal B`$ fixes every state supported in $`K`$.

*Proof.* $`\mathcal B`$ is spanned by the words in $`S_x,S_y,S_z`$ (and $`1`$); words of length $`\ge1`$ annihilate
$`K`$, so each element of $`\mathcal B`$ acts on $`K`$ as a scalar. Apply Proposition 10.1(b). $`\square`$

For $`n=4`$, Proposition 4.13 ([Chapter 4](04_symmetries.md), case $`L=4`$) gives
$`\mathcal B\cong M_5\oplus M_3\oplus M_1`$ with multiplicities $`1,3,2`$ (total spin $`2,1,0`$), so $`\dim\mathcal B=35`$ and
$`\mathcal B'\cong M_1\oplus M_3\oplus M_2`$ has dimension $`14`$. Moreover $`\dim K=m_0=2`$: the $`S_a`$ act summand by summand,
so $`K`$ is the sum of its intersections with the summands; on the spin-$`S`$ summand they act as $`S^{(S)}_a\otimes1`$
(Corollary 4.12), and
$`\sum_bu_b\otimes f_b`$ ($`f_b`$ a basis of $`\mathbb C^{m_S}`$) is annihilated by all of them iff every $`u_b`$ is annihilated by
all $`S^{(S)}_a`$. Those $`u`$ form an invariant subspace of the irreducible spin $`S`$ (Theorem 4.11), which is not everything for $`S>0`$
(as $`S^{(S)}_z\neq0`$), hence $`0`$, while for $`S=0`$ it is $`\mathbb C^1`$. Proposition 10.1(a) and its proof
hold verbatim for any $`*`$-algebra of the form $`V(\bigoplus_cM_{k_c}\otimes1_{m_c})V^*`$ that contains the Kraus
operators, here $`\mathcal B`$: four qubits carry a decoherence-free qubit ($`k=1`$, $`m=2`$) and a noiseless qutrit
($`k=3`$, $`m=3`$) for every channel whose Kraus operators lie in $`\mathcal B`$.

*Computation:* $`\dim\mathcal B'=14`$, $`\dim\mathcal B=35`$, $`\dim K=2`$, and Proposition 10.4 for random collective rotations — [tutorial, Section 10.1](../tutorial/10_quantum_computing.md#s-10-1).

## 10.2 Error correction: the Knill–Laflamme theorem

A **code** is a nonzero subspace $`\mathcal C\subseteq\mathbb C^D`$ with projection $`P`$. It **corrects** $`\Phi`$ if
there is a channel $`\mathcal R`$ (the **recovery**) with $`\mathcal R(\Phi(\rho))=\rho`$ for every state $`\rho`$
supported in $`\mathcal C`$.

<a id="r-10-5"></a>

**Theorem 10.5 (Knill–Laflamme).** $`\mathcal C`$ corrects $`\Phi`$ iff there are numbers $`\lambda_{ij}`$
with
```math
PE_i^*E_jP=\lambda_{ij}P\qquad\text{for all }i,j.
```

*Proof.* *Necessity.* Let $`\mathcal R`$ have Kraus operators $`R_m`$; then $`\mathcal R\circ\Phi`$ has the
Kraus operators $`R_mE_i`$. For a unit vector $`\psi\in\mathcal C`$,
$`\sum_{m,i}R_mE_i|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|E_i^*R_m^*=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|`$, and for $`\phi\perp\psi`$ the
expectation in $`\phi`$ gives $`\sum_{m,i}|\langle\phi,R_mE_i\psi\rangle|^2=0`$, so $`R_mE_i\psi\in\mathbb C\psi`$.
So $`R_mE_iP=c_{mi}P`$: a linear map $`T`$ with $`Tx\in\mathbb Cx`$ for all $`x`$ is a multiple of $`1`$ (if
$`Tx=ax`$, $`Ty=by`$, $`T(x+y)=c(x+y)`$, $`x,y`$ independent, then $`a=c=b`$). As $`\sum_mR_m^*R_m=1`$,
$`PE_i^*E_jP=\sum_m(R_mE_iP)^*(R_mE_jP)=\sum_m\bar c_{mi}c_{mj}P`$.

*Sufficiency.* $`\Lambda=(\lambda_{ij})`$ is Hermitian, as $`\lambda_{ji}P=(PE_i^*E_jP)^*=\bar\lambda_{ij}P`$, and
positive, as $`\sum_{ij}\bar c_ic_j\lambda_{ij}P=PF^*FP\ge0`$ for $`F=\sum_jc_jE_j`$. Write $`\Lambda=UDU^*`$,
$`U`$ unitary, $`D=\mathrm{diag}(d_k)`$, $`d_k\ge0`$ (Theorem 1.3), and $`F_k=\sum_iu_{ik}E_i`$. Then
$`\sum_kF_k\rho F_k^*=\sum_{ij}(UU^*)_{ij}E_i\rho E_j^*=\Phi(\rho)`$ and $`PF_k^*F_lP=(U^*\Lambda U)_{kl}P=d_k\delta_{kl}P`$.
If $`d_k=0`$, then $`\|F_kPx\|^2=0`$ for all $`x`$, so $`F_kP=0`$. If $`d_k>0`$, let $`V_k=F_kP/\sqrt{d_k}`$; then
$`V_k^*V_l=\delta_{kl}P`$, so $`V_kP=V_k`$, the $`Q_k=V_kV_k^*`$ are mutually orthogonal projections and
$`Q=\sum_kQ_k`$ is a projection. The recovery
```math
\mathcal R(\sigma)=\sum_{k:d_k>0}V_k^*\sigma V_k+(1-Q)\sigma(1-Q)
```
is a channel, since $`\sum_kV_kV_k^*+(1-Q)^2=1`$. For $`\rho=P\rho P`$, $`\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{d_k>0}d_kV_k\rho V_k^*`$;
since $`V_l^*V_k=\delta_{kl}P`$ and $`(1-Q)V_k=(1-Q)Q_kV_k=0`$, $`\mathcal R(\Phi(\rho))=\sum_kd_k\rho`$, and
$`\sum_kd_k=\mathrm{Tr}\,\Lambda=1`$ because $`\sum_i\lambda_{ii}P=P\sum_iE_i^*E_iP=P`$. $`\square`$

(Knill–Laflamme 1997; Nielsen–Chuang, Theorem 10.1.) $`\mathcal R`$ measures the **syndrome** — which of
the orthogonal subspaces $`Q_k\mathbb C^D`$ contains the state — and rotates the state back into the code.

**Example: the bit-flip code.** $`\mathcal C=\mathrm{span}\{|000\rangle,|111\rangle\}`$ and "at most one bit
flip", $`E_0=\sqrt{p_0}\,1`$, $`E_j=\sqrt{p_j}\,X_j`$. For $`i\neq j`$, $`X_iX_j`$ (with $`X_0=1`$) flips one or two
bits and maps $`\mathcal C`$ into $`\mathcal C^\perp`$; so $`\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(p_0,\dots,p_3)`$, $`V_k=X_kP`$,
$`Q=1`$ and $`\mathcal R(\sigma)=\sum_{k=0}^3PX_k\sigma X_kP`$. If every qubit flips independently with
probability $`p`$, the condition fails (the Kraus operators include multiples of $`X_1X_2`$ and $`X_3`$, and
$`PX_1X_2X_3P=|000\rangle\langle111|+|111\rangle\langle000|`$ is not a multiple of $`P`$), and $`\mathcal R`$ maps $`|0_L\rangle=|000\rangle`$ to $`|1_L\rangle`$ whenever
$`\ge2`$ flips occur and leaves it in place otherwise: the fidelity of $`|0_L\rangle`$ after recovery is the
probability of at most one flip, $`(1-p)^3+3p(1-p)^2=1-3p^2+2p^3=0.972`$ for $`p=0.1`$.

*Computation:* Theorem 10.5 on this example: the matrix $`\Lambda`$, the recovery $`\mathcal R`$ and the fidelity $`0.972`$ — [tutorial, Section 10.2](../tutorial/10_quantum_computing.md#s-10-2). *In the library:* channels given by Kraus operators, `channels.kraus_channel`, and their composition `R @ Phi` — [tutorial, Section 10.2](../tutorial/10_quantum_computing.md#s-10-2).

## 10.3 Operator algebra error correction

The Heisenberg picture allows one to protect less than the whole code. Let $`\mathfrak A`$ be a von
Neumann algebra on $`\mathcal C=P\mathbb C^D`$ (a $`*`$-algebra of operators on $`\mathcal C`$ containing $`P`$),
the observables to be protected. $`\mathfrak A`$ is **correctable** for $`\Phi`$ if there is a channel
$`\mathcal R`$ with
```math
P\,(\mathcal R\circ\Phi)^*(A)\,P=A\qquad\text{for all }A\in\mathfrak A,
```
i.e. $`\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal R(\Phi(\rho))A)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A)`$ for all states $`\rho`$ supported in $`\mathcal C`$
and $`A\in\mathfrak A`$ (the left side is $`\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,P(\mathcal R\circ\Phi)^*(A)P)`$, and an operator $`Y\in B(\mathcal C)`$
with $`\mathrm{Tr}(\rho Y)=0`$ for all these $`\rho`$ vanishes: $`\langle x,Yx\rangle=\langle Px,YPx\rangle=0`$ for all $`x`$, and
polarisation as in the proof of Corollary 1.4). For $`\mathfrak A=B(\mathcal C)`$ this is correction as in Section 10.2: if
$`\sigma=\mathcal R(\Phi(\rho))`$ has $`P\sigma P=\rho`$ (take all $`A\in B(\mathcal C)`$), then, as $`\mathcal R\circ\Phi`$ is trace preserving,
$`\mathrm{Tr}(1-P)\sigma(1-P)=\mathrm{Tr}\,\sigma-\mathrm{Tr}\,P\sigma P=1-1=0`$, so $`\sigma^{1/2}(1-P)=0`$ and
$`\sigma=\rho`$; conversely $`\mathcal R(\Phi(\rho))=\rho`$ gives the identity of traces. The opposite extreme is a commutative algebra: only a classical label is protected.

<a id="r-10-6"></a>

**Proposition 10.6 (correcting a sector label).** Let $`P=\sum_{c=1}^CP_c`$ with mutually orthogonal
projections $`P_c\neq0`$, and $`\mathfrak A=\mathrm{span}\{P_1,\dots,P_C\}`$. Equivalent are:
1. there are $`N_1,\dots,N_C\ge0`$, $`\sum_cN_c=1`$, with $`\mathrm{Tr}(N_c\Phi(\rho))=1`$ for every $`c`$ and
   every state $`\rho`$ supported in $`P_c\mathbb C^D`$;
2. $`P_cE_i^*E_jP_d=0`$ for all $`i,j`$ and $`c\neq d`$, i.e. $`PE_i^*E_jP\in\mathfrak A'`$, the commutant of
   $`\mathfrak A`$ in $`B(\mathcal C)`$;
3. $`\mathfrak A`$ is correctable for $`\Phi`$.

*Proof.* $`T\in B(\mathcal C)`$ commutes with all $`P_c`$ iff $`P_cTP_d=0`$ for $`c\neq d`$ (as in Corollary 10.3).

(2)$`\Rightarrow`$(1). The subspaces $`S_c=\mathrm{span}\{E_iP_cx\}`$ are mutually orthogonal, as
$`\langle E_iP_cx,E_jP_dy\rangle=\langle x,P_cE_i^*E_jP_dy\rangle=0`$. Let $`N_c`$ be the projection onto $`S_c`$ for
$`c<C`$ and $`N_C=1-\sum_{c<C}N_c`$, also a projection. For $`\rho`$ supported in $`P_c\mathbb C^D`$,
$`\Phi(\rho)=\sum_iE_iP_c\rho P_cE_i^*`$ has range in $`S_c`$, and $`N_c`$ is the identity on $`S_c`$ (for $`c=C`$
because $`S_C\perp S_{c'}`$, $`c'<C`$), so $`\mathrm{Tr}(N_c\Phi(\rho))=\mathrm{Tr}\,\Phi(\rho)=1`$.
(1)$`\Rightarrow`$(2). For a unit vector $`x\in P_c\mathbb C^D`$ and $`d\neq c`$, the numbers
$`\mathrm{Tr}(N_{d'}\Phi(|x\rangle\langle x|))\ge0`$ sum to $`1`$ and the one for $`d'=c`$ is $`1`$, so
$`0=\mathrm{Tr}(N_d\Phi(|x\rangle\langle x|))=\sum_i\|N_d^{1/2}E_ix\|^2`$; so $`N_dE_iP_c=0`$ and, summing over
$`d`$, $`N_cE_iP_c=E_iP_c`$. Hence $`P_cE_i^*E_jP_d=P_cE_i^*N_cE_jP_d=0`$ for $`c\neq d`$.
(1),(2)$`\Rightarrow`$(3). Let $`\omega_c=P_c/\mathrm{Tr}\,P_c`$ and $`\mathcal R(\sigma)=\sum_c\mathrm{Tr}(N_c\sigma)\,\omega_c`$
("measure, then prepare"), a channel with the Kraus operators $`\sqrt\mu\,|u\rangle\langle f|N_c^{1/2}`$ for the
eigenpairs $`(\mu,u)`$ of $`\omega_c`$ and the vectors $`f`$ of an orthonormal basis. Since
$`\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal R(\sigma)P_d)=\mathrm{Tr}(N_d\sigma)`$, $`\mathcal R^*(P_d)=N_d`$. Using $`N_dE_iP_c=\delta_{cd}E_iP_c`$
(from the previous step) and then $`\sum_iE_i^*E_i=1`$,
```math
P(\mathcal R\circ\Phi)^*(P_d)P=P\,\Phi^*(N_d)\,P=\sum_i\sum_{c,c'}P_{c'}E_i^*N_dE_iP_c=\sum_i\sum_{c'}P_{c'}E_i^*E_iP_d=P_d\Big(\sum_iE_i^*E_i\Big)P_d=P_d .
```
(3)$`\Rightarrow`$(1). Let $`N_c=\mathcal R^*(P_c)`$ for $`c<C`$ and $`N_C=\mathcal R^*(1-\sum_{c<C}P_c)`$; they are
positive and sum to $`\mathcal R^*(1)=1`$. For $`\rho`$ supported in $`P_c\mathbb C^D`$ and $`d<C`$,
$`\mathrm{Tr}(N_d\Phi(\rho))=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,P(\mathcal R\circ\Phi)^*(P_d)P)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho P_d)=\delta_{cd}`$, hence also
$`\mathrm{Tr}(N_C\Phi(\rho))=\delta_{cC}`$. $`\square`$

<a id="r-10-7"></a>

**Theorem 10.7 (Bény–Kempf–Kribs).** A von Neumann algebra $`\mathfrak A`$
on $`\mathcal C`$ is correctable for $`\Phi`$ iff $`PE_i^*E_jP\in\mathfrak A'`$ for all $`i,j`$, where
$`\mathfrak A'`$ is the commutant of $`\mathfrak A`$ in $`B(\mathcal C)`$.

*Proof.* This is Theorem D.9, proved in [Appendix D](D_gksl.md), whose proof also constructs a recovery
channel: it measures the sector of $`\mathfrak A\cong\bigoplus_cM_{k_c}`$ and a syndrome, and undoes the error on
each factor $`\mathbb C^{k_c}`$ as in the proof of Theorem 10.5. $`\square`$

For $`\mathfrak A=B(\mathcal C)`$, $`\mathfrak A'=\mathbb CP`$ and this is Theorem 10.5; Proposition 10.6 is the
case of the commutative $`\mathfrak A=\mathrm{span}\{P_c\}`$ (Corollary D.10(a),(b)). For
$`\mathfrak A=M_k\otimes1_m`$ on $`\mathcal C=\mathbb C^k\otimes\mathbb C^m`$ one obtains the operator (subsystem)
codes; the noiseless subsystems of Section 10.1 are such codes, corrected by $`\mathcal R=\mathrm{id}`$
(Corollary D.10(c), with the two tensor factors exchanged). By Theorem 2.6 every $`\mathfrak A`$ is
$`*`$-isomorphic to some $`\bigoplus_cM_{k_c}`$; when $`C\ge2`$ and some $`k_c\ge2`$, a correctable $`\mathfrak A`$ protects the
sector label $`c`$ together with the quantum information of each $`M_{k_c}`$ — a **hybrid** code (remark after
Corollary D.10; an example where the Knill–Laflamme condition fails but Theorem 10.7 applies is computed in
[tutorial, Section D.5](../tutorial/D_gksl.md#s-D-5)).
Historically: C. Bény, A. Kempf, D. W. Kribs, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 98, 100502 (2007) and *Phys. Rev. A* 76,
042303 (2007); operator codes: Kribs–Laflamme–Poulin, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 94, 180501 (2005).

**Example.** The repetition code under independent phase flips (probability $`q`$ per qubit) followed
by at most one bit flip. The Kraus operators are multiples of $`X_kZ^s`$, $`s\in\{0,1\}^3`$, and each
$`E_i^*E_j`$ flips at most two bits, so condition 2 holds for $`P_0=|000\rangle\langle000|`$,
$`P_1=|111\rangle\langle111|`$: the classical bit survives, read out by majority vote,
$`N_0=\sum_{|b|\le1}|b\rangle\langle b|`$, $`N_1=1-N_0`$. The qubit does not: $`PZ_1P`$ is not a multiple of $`P`$,
and after the recovery $`\mathcal R`$ of Section 10.2 the state $`|{+_L}\rangle=(|000\rangle+|111\rangle)/\sqrt2`$ suffers the logical phase flip
$`Z_1Z_2Z_3`$ exactly when an odd number of phase flips occurred; its fidelity is
$`(1+(1-2q)^3)/2=0.8645`$ for $`q=0.05`$. (The channel is $`\Phi_{\mathrm{one}}\circ\mathcal Z`$, with $`\Phi_{\mathrm{one}}`$ the
channel "at most one bit flip" of the bit-flip example and $`\mathcal Z`$ the independent phase flips;
on $`\mathcal C`$ every $`Z^s`$ acts as $`Z_1^{|s|}`$, so $`\mathcal Z(\rho)=\sum_sq^{|s|}(1-q)^{3-|s|}Z_1^{|s|}\rho Z_1^{|s|}`$ is
again supported in $`\mathcal C`$, and $`\mathcal R\circ\Phi_{\mathrm{one}}`$ is the identity on such states by Theorem 10.5.
$`Z_1`$ maps $`|{+_L}\rangle`$ to $`|{-_L}\rangle\perp|{+_L}\rangle`$, and $`|s|`$ is even with probability
$`\frac12\big((1-q+q)^3+(1-q-q)^3\big)`$.)

*Computation:* Proposition 10.6 on this example: condition 2, the majority vote $`N_0,N_1`$ and the fidelity $`0.8645`$ of $`|{+_L}\rangle`$ — [tutorial, Section 10.3](../tutorial/10_quantum_computing.md#s-10-3).

> **Physical meaning** (physical interpretation, not a theorem of this course). Protecting a commutative
> algebra means protecting classical information (a bit, the label of a superselection sector), which is
> much cheaper than protecting a qubit: noise commuting with the sector projections is harmless (then
> $`P_cE_i^*E_jP_d=E_i^*E_jP_cP_d=0`$ for $`c\neq d`$, condition 2 of Proposition 10.6).

## 10.4 Constrained mixers

The quantum alternating operator ansatz (Hadfield et al., *Algorithms* 12, 34, 2019) prepares
$`|\beta,\gamma\rangle=e^{-i\beta_pH_M}e^{-i\gamma_pH_C}\cdots e^{-i\beta_1H_M}e^{-i\gamma_1H_C}|b_0\rangle`$, $`H_C`$ a diagonal
cost Hamiltonian, $`H_M`$ a **mixer**. For a constraint $`|b|=w`$ (graph bisection, portfolios of $`w`$
assets) one takes $`|b_0|=w`$ and the **XY mixer** on a ring, $`H_M=\tfrac14\sum_{(i,j)}(X_iX_j+Y_iY_j)`$.

<a id="r-10-8"></a>

**Proposition 10.8.** $`H_M`$, $`H_C`$, $`e^{-i\beta H_M}`$ and $`e^{-i\gamma H_C}`$ lie in
$`\mathcal M=\{P_0,\dots,P_n\}'=\bigoplus_wB(H_w)\cong\bigoplus_wM_{\binom nw}`$, so $`|\beta,\gamma\rangle\in H_w`$:
the ansatz never leaves the feasible subspace.

*Proof.* On qubits $`i,j`$, $`X\otimes X+Y\otimes Y=2(|01\rangle\langle10|+|10\rangle\langle01|)`$ (use $`Y|0\rangle=i|1\rangle`$,
$`Y|1\rangle=-i|0\rangle`$): $`X_iX_j+Y_iY_j`$ swaps the bits $`i,j`$ when they differ and annihilates $`|b\rangle`$
otherwise, so it maps each $`H_w`$ into itself. A diagonal $`H_C`$ commutes with the diagonal $`P_w`$, and
so do the exponentials (Theorem 1.5(d)). $`\square`$

*In the library:* $`\mathcal M`$ is `SpinChain(n).algebra` and the XY mixer is `SpinChain.xxz` with $`\Delta=0`$ — [tutorial, Section 10.4](../tutorial/10_quantum_computing.md#s-10-4). *Computation:* Proposition 10.8 for graph bisection on six qubits, with the library and with dense matrices — [tutorial, Section 10.4](../tutorial/10_quantum_computing.md#s-10-4).

**Amplitude damping** of qubit $`i`$ ($`|1\rangle\to|0\rangle`$ with probability $`g`$) has the Kraus operators
$`K_0^{(i)}=|0\rangle\langle0|_i+\sqrt{1-g}\,|1\rangle\langle1|_i`$ and $`K_1^{(i)}=\sqrt g\,|0\rangle\langle1|_i`$, which lowers
the weight: a map between sectors.

*In the library:* `SpinChain.site_amplitude_damping`, an `InterSectorChannel` — [tutorial, Section 10.4](../tutorial/10_quantum_computing.md#s-10-4).

<a id="r-10-9"></a>

**Proposition 10.9 (leakage and post-selection).** Let $`\rho_0`$ be supported in $`H_w`$, $`U_l`$
unitaries in $`\mathcal M`$, $`\mathcal D`$ the damping of every qubit, and $`\rho_l=\mathcal D(U_l\rho_{l-1}U_l^*)`$.
Then every $`\rho_l`$ is block diagonal with weights $`\le w`$, and
```math
P_w\rho_pP_w=(1-g)^{wp}\;U\rho_0U^*,\qquad U=U_p\cdots U_1 :
```
the weight $`w`$ is found with probability $`(1-g)^{wp}`$, and the post-selected state is the noiseless one.

*Proof.* The Kraus operators of $`\mathcal D`$ are $`K_s=\prod_iK^{(i)}_{s_i}`$, $`s\in\{0,1\}^n`$; $`K_s`$ maps
$`H_v`$ into $`H_{v-|s|}`$, i.e. $`P_vK_s=K_sP_{v+|s|}`$ ($`P_v=0`$ for $`v>n`$). If $`\sigma`$ is block diagonal with
weights $`\le w`$, then $`P_vK_s\sigma K_s^*P_{v'}=K_sP_{v+|s|}\sigma P_{v'+|s|}K_s^*`$ vanishes for $`v\neq v'`$ and
for $`v>w-|s|`$: $`\mathcal D(\sigma)`$ is block diagonal with weights $`\le w`$, and its weight-$`w`$ block comes
from $`s=0`$ alone. As $`K_0|b\rangle=(1-g)^{|b|/2}|b\rangle`$, $`P_w\mathcal D(\sigma)P_w=(1-g)^wP_w\sigma P_w`$. The $`U_l`$
commute with the $`P_v`$; induct on $`l`$. $`\square`$

*Computation:* Proposition 10.9 on the bisection circuit, with post-selection by `DensityMatrix.condition_on` — [tutorial, Section 10.4](../tutorial/10_quantum_computing.md#s-10-4).

**Continuous time.** The same holds for the Lindblad equation (Theorem 8.6, [Chapter 8](08_dynamics.md))
```math
\dot\rho=-i[H,\rho]+\gamma_1\sum_i\Big(S^-_i\rho S^+_i-\tfrac12\{S^+_iS^-_i,\rho\}\Big)+\gamma_2\sum_k\Big(L_k\rho L_k^*-\tfrac12\{L_k^*L_k,\rho\}\Big)
```
with $`H\in\mathcal M`$ and jumps $`L_k\in\mathcal M`$ inside the sectors, e.g. pure dephasing $`L_k=S^z_k`$.
Here $`\sum_iS^+_iS^-_i=\sum_i|1\rangle\langle1|_i=N`$, the **number operator**, $`NP_v=vP_v`$: it is central.

<a id="r-10-10"></a>

**Proposition 10.10.** If $`\rho(0)`$ is supported in $`H_w`$, then $`P_w\rho(t)P_w=e^{-\gamma_1wt}\sigma(t)`$,
where $`\sigma`$ solves the same equation with $`\gamma_1=0`$ and $`\sigma(0)=\rho(0)`$. Hence the acceptance is
$`e^{-\gamma_1wt}`$, and the post-selected state $`\sigma(t)`$ does not depend on $`\gamma_1`$.

*Proof.* Let $`B_{vv'}=P_v\rho P_{v'}`$. As $`H,L_k,N`$ commute with the $`P_v`$ and $`P_vS^-_i=S^-_iP_{v+1}`$,
$`\dot B_{vv'}=-i[H,B_{vv'}]+\gamma_1\sum_iS^-_iB_{v+1,v'+1}S^+_i-\tfrac{\gamma_1}2(v+v')B_{vv'}+\gamma_2\mathcal L_2(B_{vv'})`$,
$`\mathcal L_2`$ the dephasing part. The blocks with $`\max(v,v')>w`$ satisfy a closed linear system with
zero initial values, so they vanish (uniqueness in Lemma 8.1). Then $`\dot B_{ww}=-i[H,B_{ww}]-\gamma_1wB_{ww}+\gamma_2\mathcal L_2(B_{ww})`$, so
$`e^{\gamma_1wt}B_{ww}`$ solves the equation of $`\sigma`$ with the same initial value, and equals $`\sigma`$
(Lemma 8.1). That equation is again of GKSL form, so $`\mathrm{Tr}\,\sigma(t)=1`$ (Theorem 8.6). $`\square`$

Compare Proposition 8.11, which allows a non-central $`\sum_jL_j^*L_j`$ but no jumps inside the sectors: here
the loss term $`\gamma_1N`$ is central, so the no-jump factor is the scalar $`e^{-\gamma_1wt}`$ and dephasing
inside the sector is allowed.

For $`\gamma_2=0`$, $`\sigma(t)=e^{-iHt}\rho(0)e^{iHt}`$: post-selection is perfect.

*Computation:* Proposition 10.10 for the XY mixer on six qubits, with `dynamics.lindblad_evolve` — [tutorial, Section 10.4](../tutorial/10_quantum_computing.md#s-10-4).

> **Physical meaning** (physical interpretation, not a theorem of this course). Energy relaxation ($`T_1`$), a leading error of superconducting qubits, is a
> jump to a lower sector. Measuring the Hamming weight — a central projection, harmless inside a
> sector (Section 3.3) — detects it, and post-selection removes it at the price of the acceptance
> rate. Dephasing commutes with the sector projections and is not detected.
