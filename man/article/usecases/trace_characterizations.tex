\subsection{Use case: quantitative failure of trace characterisations}\label{sec:tc}

\paragraph{The question.} Many inequalities that hold for the trace also characterise it. Gardner~\cite{gardner1979}
showed that a positive functional $\varphi$ on a C$^*$-algebra with $|\varphi(X)|\le\varphi(|X|)$ for all $X$ is a
trace. Monotonicity, Young, Araki--Lieb--Thirring and determinant inequalities, inequalities for projections, for
$|\varphi(A^2)|$ and for the spectral geometric mean have since been shown to single out traces as well, on $M_n$
or among the positive normal functionals on a von Neumann
algebra~\cite{petz1988,stolyarov2002,bikchentaev2005young,bikchentaev2007mono,bikchentaev2011pb,bikchentaev2019abed,bikchentaev2019metric,bikchentaev2020det,bikchentaev2026moslehian,bikchentaev2026sgm}.
So have subadditivity~\cite{tikhonov2005}, the weighted monotonicity inequality $\varphi(A^{2r+2})\le\varphi(A^rB^2A^r)$
for $0\le A\le B$~\cite{hoa2007,hoa2010}, the generalised Powers--St{\o}rmer inequality~\cite{hoa2013}, the
subadditivity $\varphi(P\vee Q)\le\varphi(P)+\varphi(Q)$ on the projection lattice~\cite[Theorem~4]{petz1988}, and a
family of inequalities restricted to projections $P,Q$ (H\"older, Cauchy--Schwarz, Golden--Thompson, $\varphi(PQP)\le
\varphi(Q)$, $\varphi(\mathrm{Re}\,PQ)\ge0$, \dots)~\cite{bikchentaev2010smj,bikchentaev2011mn}. For Young's
inequality see also~\cite{cho2009}; Bikchentaev and Moslehian~\cite{bikchentaev2025survey} survey these
characterisations. These theorems answer a yes/no question. We ask how strongly an inequality $L\le R$ fails for a
given non-tracial functional.

On $M_n$ every positive functional has the form $\varphi_W=\Tr(W\,\cdot\,)$ with $W\ge0$, and it is a trace iff $W$
is scalar. We write $a\ge b>0$ for the extreme eigenvalues of $W$, $h=a/b=(1+\delta)/(1-\delta)$ with
$\delta=\Delta(W)$, $\kappa=(a+b)/(2\sqrt{ab})=(1-\delta^2)^{-1/2}$ for the Kantorovich constant, and $\psi=\arcsin\delta$.
The \emph{violation envelope} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tcV}
  V(\delta)=\sup\bigl\{(L-R)/N:\ \Delta(W)=\delta,\ \text{all operator variables free}\bigr\},
\end{equation}
where $N$ is a normalisation that makes $V$ independent of the scales of $W$ and of the variables (mostly $N=R$).

\paragraph{Sectors.} On $\M=\bigoplus_cM_{k_c}$ the traces are $\sum_ct_c\Tr_c$, $t_c\ge0$, so $\varphi_W$ is a trace
iff $W$ is central, whatever its inter-block contrast. All items except 11 and 24 hold for every trace of $\M$, and
except in item~16 both sides are sums over the blocks (in item~14 use H\"older's inequality over the blocks). Hence
$V_\M(W)\le\max_cV_{M_{k_c}}(W_c)$, with equality when $N$ is additive and the variables may vanish on all but one
block (items 1--10, 12--14, 17, 18, 21--23, 25); in items 15, 19 and 20, $N$ contains $\varphi(1)$, and the other
blocks dilute the violation. The violation is governed by the
\emph{intra-block} contrasts alone, whatever reference trace is used to write $\varphi=\Tr(W\,\cdot\,)$. Items 11 and
24 characterise the \emph{canonical} trace among functionals with $\varphi(1)=n$, and central $W$ violate them. The
script checks this on $M_2\oplus M_3$ with $W=1\oplus s1$ and $W=\mathrm{diag}(1,\ell)\oplus s1$, for
$s\in\{\frac14,4\}$ and intra-block contrast $\delta\in\{0.3,0.5,0.7\}$.
\IfFileExists{generated/trace_characterizations.tex}{For central $W$ the largest value found for the items that hold
for every trace is $\TCSectorCentral$. For items 11 and 24 the search reproduces the exact values for the fixed

cases.}{} A density that varies only between sectors is invisible to every characterisation except 11 and~24.

\paragraph{Closed forms.} The constants rest on classical inequalities: $\kappa$ enters through the Kantorovich
inequality $\omega(W^2)\,\omega(1)\le\kappa^2\omega(W)^2$ for positive functionals $\omega$ and $b\le W\le
a$~\cite{greub1959,furuta2005}, $h$ through $b\le W\le a$, and the Specht ratio through Specht's reverse AM--GM
inequality. What the following propositions add is that the resulting constants are sharp for the inequalities
characterising the trace, i.e.\ the values of the envelopes~\eqref{eq:tcV}.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:tcK}
Let $W>0$ in $M_n$, $n\ge2$. For all $X,Y,A$ and all projections $P,Q,R$, with $\varphi=\varphi_W$:
$|\varphi(X)|\le\kappa\varphi(|X|)$, $\varphi(A+A^*)\le2\kappa\varphi(|A^*|)$, $|\varphi(A^2)|\le\kappa\varphi(A^*A)$,
$\varphi(|X+Y|)\le\kappa(\varphi(|X|)+\varphi(|Y|))$ and $\varphi(|P-R|)\le\kappa(\varphi(|P-Q|)+\varphi(|Q-R|))$.
All five constants are attained, so $V=\kappa-1$ for items 1--5, in every dimension.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $X=U|X|$ and $P=|X|$. Then $\varphi(X)=\langle WP^{1/2},UP^{1/2}\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}$, so $|\varphi(X)|^2\le
\Tr(W^2P)\Tr P$ for every contraction $U$, and $\Tr(W^2P)\Tr P\le\kappa^2(\Tr WP)^2$ is the Kantorovich inequality, in
the form of Greub and Rheinboldt~\cite{greub1959}, for $\omega=\Tr(P\,\cdot\,)$ (its standard proof: $(W-a)(W-b)\le0$ gives $\Tr(W^2P)\Tr P\le((a+b)x-aby)y\le\kappa^2x^2$
with $x=\Tr WP$ and $y=\Tr P$, the difference being $(\kappa x-\sqrt{ab}\,y)^2$). The second inequality is the same,
with $A=|A^*|V$. For the fourth, write $X+Y=S|X+Y|$, so that $\varphi(|X+Y|)=\varphi(S^*X)+\varphi(S^*Y)$, and bound
each term with the contraction $S^*U_X$ or $S^*U_Y$. The fifth is the fourth with $X=P-Q$ and $Y=Q-R$. For the
third, put $X=W^{1/2}A$ in an eigenbasis of $W$. Then $\varphi(A^*A)=\sum|X_{ij}|^2$, while
$\varphi(A^2)=\sum_iX_{ii}^2+\sum_{i<j}X_{ij}X_{ji}(w_i+w_j)/\sqrt{w_iw_j}$ and $(w_i+w_j)/\sqrt{w_iw_j}\le2\kappa$.
Equality holds as follows, with $e_1,e_2$ eigenvectors of $W$ for $a,b$: in the first two for $P=vv^*$ with
$|\langle e_1,v\rangle|^2=\frac b{a+b}$, $|\langle e_2,v\rangle|^2=\frac a{a+b}$ and $Uv=Wv/\|Wv\|$; in the
third for $A=\sqrt a\,e_1e_2^*+\sqrt b\,e_2e_1^*$; in the fourth for $X=uu^*$, $Y=-u'u'^*$ with
$u,u'=\sin t\,e_1\pm\cos t\,e_2$ and $\tan t=\sqrt{b/a}$; in the fifth for $P=uu^*$, $Q=0$, $R=u'u'^*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:tcB}
(a) $\varphi_W(|AB^*|)\le h\cdot\frac12(\varphi_W(|A|^2)+\varphi_W(|B|^2))$, with equality for $A=B=e_1e_2^*$; so
$V=h-1=2\delta/(1-\delta)$ for item~8, the extension of Young's inequality to arbitrary $A,B$, for every $n$. (b) If
$\Tr W=n$, item~11 has $V=1-\mathrm{GM}/\mathrm{AM}$ of the eigenvalues of $W$, and the maximum over spectra in
$[b,a]$ is attained at two-level spectra:
$V_n(\delta)=1-\min_{1\le m<n}\ell^{(n-m)/n}n/(m+(n-m)\ell)$, $\ell=1/h$. In particular $V_2=1-\sqrt{1-\delta^2}$,
and $V_n\uparrow1-1/S(h)$, where $S$ is the Specht ratio. (c) For $n=2$, item~9 has $V=s/(1+s)$ with
$s=\sin(\psi/2)$; thus $V\sim\delta/2$ as $\delta\to0$ and $V\to\sqrt2-1$ as $\delta\to1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) $\varphi_W(|AB^*|)\le a\|A\|_2\|B\|_2$ and $\varphi_W(|A|^2)\ge b\|A\|_2^2$. (b) By the classical AM--GM argument
for the eigenvalues of $W^{1/2}AW^{1/2}$, $\inf_{A>0}\varphi_W(A)/(n(\det A)^{1/n})=(\det W)^{1/n}$, attained at
$A=W^{-1}$. In coordinates $x_i=\log w_i$, $\mathrm{AM}/\mathrm{GM}=\frac1n\sum
e^{x_i-\bar x}$ is convex, so it is maximal at a vertex of the box; the limit is Specht's theorem. (c) Ranks 0 and
2 give no violation. For rank-one $P,Q$ the ratio reduces to $r/(1+r)$ with an explicit $r$ depending on two
angles, whose maximum is $r=\sin(\psi/2)$ (computation in the catalogue file).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:tcC}
For every $n\ge2$: $V_6\ge\kappa-1$, $V_7\ge\kappa^2-1=\delta^2/(1-\delta^2)$ (both approached, not attained), and
$V_{10}\ge m(\delta):=\max_{0<y<\delta}y^2(\delta-y)^2/((1-2\delta y+y^2)(1-y^2))=\delta^4/16+O(\delta^5)$
(attained). Conversely, $V\le h-1$ for items 1--8 and 10.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $e_\pm=(e_1\pm e_2)/\sqrt2$. (6) For invertible $A$ and $B=vv^*$, $A\natural B=(v^*Av)^{1/2}vv^*$, because
$A^{-1}\#B=vv^*/(v^*Av)^{1/2}$. Taking $v=e_+$ and $A=xx^*+\varepsilon$ with $x\propto W^{-1}v$, the ratio
$\varphi(A\natural B)/\sqrt{\varphi(A)\varphi(B)}$ tends to $((v^*W^{-1}v)(v^*Wv))^{1/2}=\kappa$. (7) Take $B\to$ rank
one, $B=\eta f_1f_1^*+f_2f_2^*$ in the basis $f_{1,2}=e_\pm$, and $A=uu^*$ with $u=f_1+s\eta f_2$; as $\eta\to0$ the
ratio tends to $\kappa^2$ at the best $s$. (10) Rank-one $A=uu^*$ and $B-A=tvv^*$ with $u,v$ at angles
$\alpha,\pi-\alpha$ to $e_1$ and $y=-\cos2\alpha$ give $m(\delta)$ (both computations in the catalogue file). (Upper
bound) In each item $L\le aL_0$, with $L_0$ the trace or trace-norm version of $L$ (e.g.\ $|\varphi(X)|\le a\|X\|_1$);
moreover $L_0\le R_{\Tr}$ and $R\ge bR_{\Tr}$. For item~6, $\Tr(A\natural B)=\|A^{1/2}B^{1/2}\|_1\le(\Tr A\Tr B)^{1/2}$.
\end{proof}

Items 17--25 behave as follows; the proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:tc}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:tcP}
Let $W>0$ in $M_n$, $n\ge2$, and let $P,Q$ be projections. With $N=R$ except $N=\varphi(P)+\varphi(Q)$ in item~21:
$V=\kappa^2-1$ for $\varphi(PQP)\le\varphi(Q)$ (17); $V=\kappa-1$ for $\varphi(|P-Q|)\le\varphi(P+Q)$ (18) and for
$\varphi(\mathrm{Re}\,PQ)\le\varphi(|PQ|)$ (22); $V=\delta$ for $\varphi(P+Q)\le\varphi(1+P\wedge Q)$ (19);
$V=\delta/(1-\delta)$ for $\varphi(P\vee Q)\le\varphi(P)+\varphi(Q)$ (23); $V=\frac12\tan^2(\psi/2)$ for
$\varphi(\mathrm{Re}\,PQ)\ge0$ (21); and for $\varphi(e^{QPQ})\le\varphi(e^P)$ (20), $V=\max_{0<g<\pi}\lambda(\delta,g)$,
the largest root of an explicit quadratic, $=\frac{(e-1)^2}{e(e+1)}\delta^2+O(\delta^3)$. The values of 17, 18 and
21--23 hold in every dimension, those of 19 and 20 for even $n$. For odd $n$, where $N$ contains $\varphi(1)$ and a
one-dimensional block is left over, $V_{19}=(n-1)\delta/(n-\delta)$, and $V_{20}$ lies between $V_{20}$ for $n=2$ and
the same maximum with $\varphi(1)$ increased by $(1-\delta)/(n-1)$ in~$N$. The values are attained in 17, 18, 20--22
by rank-one $P,Q$, and approached in 19 and 23 as $P\ne Q$ tend to the same eigenvector of $W$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:tcL}
If $\Tr W=n$, the envelope of $\varphi(A^2)^{1/2}\le\varphi(A)$, $A\ge0$ (item~24, the case $p=2$, $q=1$
of~\cite[Theorem~3(vii)]{bikchentaev2020det}), is $w_{\min}^{-1/2}-1$ for a fixed $W$ and
$V_n(\delta)=\sqrt{(\ell+n-1)/(n\ell)}-1$, $\ell=1/h$, at contrast $\delta$: $V_2=(1-\delta)^{-1/2}-1\sim\delta/2$,
$V_n\sim\frac{n-1}n\delta$, and $V_n\uparrow\sqrt h-1$ as $n\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:tcW}
For $\varphi(A^4)\le\varphi(AB^2A)$, $0\le A\le B$ (item~25, the case $r=1$ of~\cite[Theorems~4, 5]{hoa2010}),
rank-one $A$ gives no violation, and an explicit construction with $A$ of rank two and $B-A$ of rank one gives
$V_{25}\ge m_{25}(\delta)=\delta^8/4096\,(1+O(\delta^2))$ in every dimension.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Computation and validation.} $W$ is diagonal with the spectrum of \texttt{optimize.SpectrumParam}, so that
$\Delta(W)=\delta$ is exact and the other eigenvalues are free. The variables are \texttt{PositiveParam} (free
contrast in $[0,1]$, so rank-deficient operators are included), \texttt{SelfAdjointParam}, \texttt{UnitaryParam}
(polar factors, and projections $UDU^*$ with every rank pattern spread over the starts), and positive scalars. Half
of the starts begin at the structured points of \texttt{optimize}. The lattice operations are spectral projections of
$P+Q$ (eigenvalue $2$ for $P\wedge Q$, range for $P\vee Q$), computed with \texttt{apply\_function} and an indicator
function, whose Daleckii--Krein gradient is the derivative of the spectral projection. The
\IfFileExists{generated/trace_characterizations.tex}{\TCGridSize}{14} contrasts times
\IfFileExists{generated/trace_characterizations.tex}{\TCStarts}{the} starts form one batch for
\texttt{extremize}\IfFileExists{generated/trace_characterizations.tex}{ (\TCSteps\ steps, $n=\TCDims$)}{}. The
program \repofile{trace_characterizations.py} does this for item~1 on $M_2$ at five contrasts in one batch and
reproduces $V=\kappa-1$ (\texttt{optimize.SpectrumParam} with \texttt{delta}, \texttt{PositiveParam},
\texttt{UnitaryParam}, \texttt{extremize}). In the script, the best final point of every contrast is recomputed in
mpmath with
\IfFileExists{generated/trace_characterizations.tex}{\TCDigits}{40} digits, after the unitaries are re-ortho\-normalised,
and $\Delta(W)$ is recomputed from the stored eigenvalues; the lattice operations are recomputed there from the exact
eigenvalues. Every positive entry of Table~\ref{tab:tc} is thus a certified violation at an exactly admissible point.
\IfFileExists{generated/trace_characterizations.tex}{At $\delta=0$ no value exceeds $\TCVzero$. The double-precision
and mpmath values agree to $\TCCertDev$ (relative). Where $V$ is known exactly and attained (items 1--5, 8, 11, and 9

\paragraph{Results.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Detectability.} $V(\delta)>0$ for every $\delta>0$ in all items, by the constructions above and,
for 12--16, by certified search values.
\item[(ii)] \emph{Strength.} The strength varies by orders of magnitude (Table~\ref{tab:tcclass}). As $\delta\to0$,
five inequalities fail linearly: the extended Young inequality (8), the projection inequality (9), the two lattice
inequalities (19, 23) and the power-mean inequality for the canonical trace (24). Most fail quadratically: items
1--5, 18 and 22 as $\kappa-1\sim\delta^2/2$, item 17 as $\kappa^2-1\sim\delta^2$, item 21 as $\delta^2/8$, item 20 as
$0.29\,\delta^2$, item 11 as $\delta^2/2$, items 6 and 7 at least as $\delta^2/2$ and $\delta^2$, and in the search
items 12--16 with coefficients between $1/36$ and about $1/2$. Monotonicity (10) fails at least as $\delta^4/16$ and
its weighted form with $r=1$ (25) at least as $\delta^8/4096$; the search finds nothing larger.
\IfFileExists{generated/trace_characterizations.tex}{At $\delta=0.1$ the violations range from
$\TCVYoungTenth$ (item~8) to $\TCRefWmonTenth$ (item~25, the construction), and at $\delta=0.5$ item~25 gives only
$\TCRefWmonHalf$.}{} Two inequalities that are equivalent for traces can behave very differently: by the
parallelogram law $\varphi(P\vee Q)+\varphi(P\wedge Q)=\varphi(P)+\varphi(Q)$ for a trace, yet item~23 fails as
$\delta/(1-\delta)$ and item~18, with the same right side, as $\kappa-1$. As $\delta\to1$, the closed forms tend
to $\infty$ except for items 9, 11, 19 and 21, which tend to $\sqrt2-1$, $1$, $1$ and $\frac12$, and item~20, which is
at most $e-1$ ($N\ge\varphi(1)$, $L\le e\varphi(1)$).
\item[(iii)] \emph{Dimension.} Items 1--5, 8, 17, 18 and 21--23 do not depend on $n$, nor do 19 and 20 among
even $n$ (proved); for odd $n$ the violation of 19 and 20 is diluted by a left-over one-dimensional block. For items 9 and 10 the
search gives the same values for $n=3,4$ as for $n=2$, to
\IfFileExists{generated/trace_characterizations.tex}{$\TCNDev$}{round-off}. Items 11 and 24 grow with $n$ (to the
Specht limit and to $\sqrt h-1$). Item~6 depends on $n$: for $n=2$ the search never exceeds $\kappa-1$, but for
$n\ge3$ it certifies larger violations:
\IfFileExists{generated/trace_characterizations.tex}{$V\ge\TCSGMExcessV$ at $n=\TCSGMExcessN$,
$\delta=\TCSGMExcessDelta$ ($\kappa-1=\TCSGMExcessKappa$)}{}, and a stored point in the tests gives $V>2.1$ at $n=3$,
$\delta=0.9$, where $\kappa-1=1.29$. We only know $\kappa-1\le V_6\le h-1$.
\item[(iv)] \emph{Extremisers.} Except for items 6 ($n\ge3$), 11 ($A=W^{-1}$) and 25 ($A$ of rank two with
eigenvalue ratio $\approx\delta^2/8$), the extremisers are rank-one operators or matrix units in the span of
eigenvectors for $a$ and $b$, and they do not commute with $W$. In 19 and 23 the two projections approach the same
eigenvector of $W$ ($a$ resp.\ $b$) without coinciding, so that $P\wedge Q=0$ and $P\vee Q$ is two-dimensional.
\item[(v)] \emph{Limits of the search.} The suprema of items 6 and 7 are approached only as $B$ becomes singular.
Gradient ascent reaches
\IfFileExists{generated/trace_characterizations.tex}{$\TCSGMRatioHalf$ and $\TCALTRatioHalf$}{part} of the bound at
$\delta=0.5$, and it finds no violation of item~6 for $\delta\le0.2$. Item~25 is resolved only for
\IfFileExists{generated/trace_characterizations.tex}{$\delta\ge\TCWmonFirst$, and it reaches $m_{25}$ for
$\delta\ge\TCWmonReach$}{large $\delta$}.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Observation (conjecture):} $V_7=\kappa^2-1$, $V_{10}=m(\delta)$ and $V_{25}=m_{25}(\delta)$ in every
dimension, $V_6=\kappa-1$ for $n=2$, and item~9 equals Proposition~\ref{prop:tcB}(c) for all $n$.

\paragraph{What is new.} The characterisation theorems are due to the cited authors. The constants $\kappa$, $h$ and
the Specht ratio and the inequalities behind them are classical; the bounds of Proposition~\ref{prop:tcK} are the
Kantorovich inequality~\cite{greub1959,furuta2005}, and only their sharpness for items 1--5 is ours. We found no
study of the envelopes~\eqref{eq:tcV}. The closest work is~\cite{abed2024}: its Monte Carlo study
(\cite[Sect.~5.1, Fig.~1]{abed2024}) plots the unnormalised violation $|\Tr(XY)|-\Tr(X|Y|)$ of Gardner's
inequality for $\varphi=\Tr(X\,\cdot\,)$ against the contrast $\Delta(X)$ of the density, for real $X,Y$ with
$\|X\|=\|Y\|=1$ and $n=2,3$, and conjectures that such violations have upper bounds depending on Michelson
contrasts~\cite[Sect.~7]{abed2024}. It defines no envelope, proves no bound, and treats only item~1 (its exact
unnormalised envelope is the case $k=2$, $C=1$ of Exp.~1 in Section~\ref{sec:optimisation}). As far as we can
tell, the following are new: the sharp values for items 1--5, 8 (in the extended form), 11, 9 with $n=2$, 17, 18,
21--24, and 19, 20 for even $n$; the bounds of Propositions~\ref{prop:tcC} and~\ref{prop:tcW}; the classification by order of vanishing
($\delta$, $\delta^2$, $\delta^4$, $\delta^8$); the leading order of item~14; the dependence of item~6 on $n$; and
the fact that characterisations of traces detect only intra-block contrast. The rest are numerical observations.
