# Tutorial 4. Symmetries and representations

This tutorial accompanies [Chapter 4](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md) of the lecture notes, which develops the representation
theory behind the algebras of symmetries, turns it into a randomised algorithm that decomposes the von Neumann algebra
generated by any list of matrices ([Proposition 4.9](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-9)), applies it to $`U(1)`$ and $`SU(2)`$, and sorts the
sectors of an algebra generated by real matrices into real, complex and quaternionic type
([Theorem 4.20](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-20)). We compute characters, run the decomposition on groups and on generators, build spin
chains sector by sector with `SpinChain` and `su2.SU2Chain`, and compute the real structure of a decomposition.

## 4.2 The isotypic decomposition and the commutant

*Lecture notes:* [Section 4.2](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#s-4-2); [Theorem 4.3](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-3), [Theorem 4.4](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-4), [Corollary 4.5](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-5).

[Section 4.2](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#s-4-2) of the lecture notes computes by hand the characters of three irreducible representations
of $`S_3`$ — the trivial one, the sign and the standard representation on $`(1,1,1)^\perp\subseteq\mathbb C^3`$ — and of the
representation $`\pi`$ permuting the three factors of $`(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes3}`$, $`\chi_\pi(\sigma)=2^{\#\text{cycles}(\sigma)}`$
(table below), and obtains from [Corollary 4.5](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-5) the multiplicities $`m_{\rm triv}=4`$, $`m_{\rm sgn}=0`$,
$`m_{\rm std}=2`$ and $`\dim\pi(S_3)'=20`$. The code recomputes the table: it finds $`P_\sigma`$ as the restriction of
$`\pi(\sigma)`$ to the invariant span of $`|100\rangle,|010\rangle,|001\rangle`$ (on which $`\pi(\sigma)`$ permutes the positions
of the single $`1`$, Lemma B.1(a)), and $`\#\text{cycles}(\sigma)=\dim\ker(P_\sigma-1)`$ (the vectors constant on the cycles).

| | identity ($`1`$) | transpositions ($`3`$) | $`3`$-cycles ($`2`$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $`\chi_{\rm triv}`$ | $`1`$ | $`1`$ | $`1`$ |
| $`\mathrm{sgn}`$ | $`1`$ | $`-1`$ | $`1`$ |
| $`\chi_{\rm std}`$ | $`2`$ | $`0`$ | $`-1`$ |
| $`\chi_\pi`$ | $`8`$ | $`4`$ | $`2`$ |

```python
import math
import torch
import torch_vn_algebra.decompose as dc

torch.manual_seed(0)
dt = torch.complex128

def close(X, Y, tol=1e-10):
    return (torch.as_tensor(X) - torch.as_tensor(Y)).abs().max().item() < tol

G = dc.permutation_representation(3, 2)                  # (6, 8, 8): pi(sigma) permutes three qubits
chi_pi = G.diagonal(dim1=-2, dim2=-1).sum(-1).real       # characters Tr pi(sigma)
P3 = G[:, [4, 2, 1]][:, :, [4, 2, 1]].real              # on |100>, |010>, |001>: S_3 permuting C^3
cycles = 3 - torch.linalg.matrix_rank(P3 - torch.eye(3, dtype=torch.float64)).double()   # dim ker(P - 1)
assert close(chi_pi, 2.0 ** cycles)
irreps = {'trivial': torch.ones(6, dtype=torch.float64), 'sign': (-1.0) ** (3 - cycles),
          'standard': P3.diagonal(dim1=-2, dim2=-1).sum(-1) - 1}                 # fixed points - 1
chis = torch.stack(list(irreps.values()))
assert close(chis @ chis.T / 6, torch.eye(3, dtype=torch.float64))    # Corollary 4.5(a): all irreducible
mult = chis @ chi_pi / 6                                               # Corollary 4.5(b)
print(dict(zip(irreps, mult.tolist())), (chi_pi ** 2).mean().item())
assert close(mult, torch.tensor([4.0, 0.0, 2.0], dtype=torch.float64))
assert dc.commutant_dimension(G) == 20                                # Corollary 4.5(c): M_4 (+) M_2
```

This is the decomposition of Section 2.4, now obtained from a table of six numbers per representation.

## 4.3 From generators to the algebra: a randomised algorithm

*Lecture notes:* [Section 4.3](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#s-4-3); [Lemma 4.6](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-6), [Proposition 4.7](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-7), [Lemma 4.8](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-8), [Proposition 4.9](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-9).

The randomised algorithm of the lecture notes is `decompose.wedderburn`, with the front ends `from_group` (all
elements of a finite group; the commutant is sampled with the twirl `decompose.twirl`) and `from_generators` (any list
of matrices; the commutant is a null space, `decompose.commutant_basis`). It splits $`\mathbb C^n`$ into the eigenspaces
of a random $`K\in\mathcal A'_h`$, groups and aligns them with the blocks of a random $`Y\in\mathcal A'`$, and returns a
`Decomposition` with the unitary `V`, the $`k_c`$ (`k_factors`) and the $`m_c`$ (`multiplicities`). In floating point it
treats eigenvalues closer than a tolerance as equal and splits the resulting subspaces further by compressing fresh
random elements of the commutant to them ([Lemma 4.8(a)](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-8)).

`Decomposition.verify()` reports the relative distances of the generators (and of fresh elements of the
commutant) from $`\mathcal B`$ (resp. $`\mathcal B'`$), and compares $`\sum_cm_c^2`$ with $`\dim\mathcal A'`$ (from the
characters for a group, Corollary 4.5(c); from the null space for generators). We run it on $`S_3`$ and three
qubits, and then look inside: the eigenspaces of one random $`K`$ and the intertwiners of one random $`Y`$.

```python
D = dc.from_group(G)                                     # the twirl samples pi(S_3)'
print(D)
assert D.k_factors == (1, 2) and D.multiplicities == (4, 2)            # Section 2.4: k = (1, 2), m = (4, 2)
assert D.verify()['ok']
rho = D.to_algebra(G).matrix                             # (6, 2, 2, 2): the blocks rho_c(sigma), padded
assert close(rho[:, 0, 0, 0], torch.ones(6))                           # sector 1: trivial representation
assert close(rho[:, 1].diagonal(dim1=-2, dim2=-1).sum(-1), chis[2])    # sector 2: the standard one
schur = torch.einsum('gij,gkl->ijkl', rho[:, 1], rho[:, 1].conj()) / 6
assert close(schur.reshape(4, 4), torch.eye(4) / 2)                    # Theorem 4.4 with k = 2
assert close(D.from_algebra(D.to_algebra(G)), G)

Z = torch.randn(8, 8, dtype=dt)
K = dc.twirl(G, Z + Z.mH)                                # a random element of pi(S_3)'_h
w, Q = torch.linalg.eigh(K)
cuts = [0] + [i + 1 for i in range(7) if w[i + 1] - w[i] > 1e-8] + [8]
spaces = [Q[:, a:b] for a, b in zip(cuts[:-1], cuts[1:])]
assert sorted(W.shape[1] for W in spaces) == [1, 1, 1, 1, 2, 2]      # Lemma 4.6: m_c pieces of size k_c
assert all(close(G @ W, W @ (W.mH @ G @ W)) for W in spaces)           # invariant subspaces
Y = dc.twirl(G, torch.randn(8, 8, dtype=dt))
Wa, Wb = [W for W in spaces if W.shape[1] == 2]
s = torch.linalg.svdvals(Wa.mH @ Y @ Wb)                               # Lemma 4.8(b): a multiple of a unitary
assert s[1] > 1e-3 and abs(s[0] - s[1]) < 1e-10
assert all(close(W.mH @ Y @ Wa, 0) for W in spaces if W.shape[1] == 1)   # different summands: zero
```

The commutant dominates the cost: a twirl sample costs $`|G|`$ products of $`n\times n`$ matrices. For generators
the library first diagonalises a Hermitian element $`R=U\mathrm{diag}(\lambda)U^*`$ of $`\mathcal A`$ (a random real
combination of the Hermitian parts of the generators plus a multiple of a symmetrised product of two such
combinations). Every $`X\in\mathcal A'`$ commutes with $`R`$, hence with its spectral projections (Theorem 1.5(d)), so
$`U^*XU`$ is block diagonal with respect to the eigenspaces of $`R`$, of dimensions $`d_\lambda`$; this is imposed in
advance, and the null-space problem has $`N_R=\sum_\lambda d_\lambda^2`$ unknowns instead of $`n^2`$. Grouping several
eigenvalues together only enlarges the blocks, so the reduced problem has the same solution set $`\mathcal A'`$.
In the decomposition, $`V^*RV=\bigoplus_cr_c\otimes1_{m_c}`$ with Hermitian $`r_c\in M_{k_c}`$; if $`\lambda`$ has multiplicity
$`\mu_c(\lambda)`$ in $`r_c`$, then $`d_\lambda=\sum_c\mu_c(\lambda)m_c`$, and since $`\mu^2\ge\mu`$ for integers $`\mu\ge0`$,
```math
N_R=\sum_\lambda\Big(\sum_c\mu_c(\lambda)m_c\Big)^2\ge\sum_{\lambda,c}\mu_c(\lambda)^2m_c^2\ge\sum_{\lambda,c}\mu_c(\lambda)m_c^2=\sum_ck_cm_c^2,
```
with equality iff the eigenvalues of all the $`r_c`$ are simple and pairwise distinct. For the total spin of
$`L=3,4,5`$ spins the code below finds equality (numerical observation). Even the minimum is large: for eight spins
$`\frac12`$ ($`n=256`$) $`\sum_ck_cm_c^2=4900`$, and the reduced system, an $`(n^2+N_R)\times N_R`$ matrix, has $`3.5\cdot10^8`$
complex entries. For spin chains Section 4.6 therefore uses the module `su2`, which knows the answer in advance.

```python
for n in (3, 4, 5):
    Dn = dc.from_generators(dc.total_spin(n))
    _, info = dc.commutant_basis(dc.total_spin(n), return_info=True)
    N_min = sum(k * m * m for k, m in zip(Dn.k_factors, Dn.multiplicities))
    assert info['unknowns'] >= N_min                                  # N_R >= sum_c k_c m_c^2
    print(n, info['unknowns'], N_min)                                # observed: equality
```

## 4.4 $`U(1)`$ and charge sectors

*Lecture notes:* [Section 4.4](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#s-4-4); [Proposition 4.10](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-10).

For $`n`$ qubits the charge is the number operator $`\hat N=\sum_jn_j`$, $`n_j=|1\rangle\langle1|`$ on qubit $`j`$, and the operators that conserve it form
$`U(1)'\cong\bigoplus_{w=0}^nM_{\binom nw}(\mathbb C)`$ ([lecture notes, Section 4.4](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#s-4-4)), which is
`chains.SpinChain(n).algebra` (bit $`1`$ = spin up, $`\hat N=S^z+\frac n2`$; in `decompose.total_spin` spin up is instead
the *first* basis vector of $`\mathbb C^2`$, which relabels the basis but not the sectors). Every XXZ Hamiltonian lies in
$`U(1)'`$. The code finds the sectors from $`\hat N`$ alone and diagonalises an XXZ ring of four spins sector by sector.

```python
import functools
from torch_vn_algebra import SpinChain

L = 4
Sx, Sy, Sz = dc.total_spin(L)                              # (16, 16) each, site 0 most significant
N_op = Sz + L / 2 * torch.eye(2 ** L, dtype=dt)            # number of up spins
DU = dc.from_generators(N_op)                              # the algebra generated by U(1)
print(DU)
chain = SpinChain(L, boundary='periodic')                  # U(1)' = (+)_w M_binom(L, w)
assert set(DU.k_factors) == {1} and sorted(DU.multiplicities) == sorted(chain.algebra.k_factors)
assert sorted(chain.algebra.k_factors) == sorted(math.comb(L, w) for w in range(L + 1))

sx, sy, sz = dc.spin_matrices(0.5)
def two_site(a, b, i, j, n=L):                             # a on site i, b on site j of n sites
    ops = [a if k == i else b if k == j else torch.eye(2, dtype=dt) for k in range(n)]
    return functools.reduce(torch.kron, ops)

H = sum(two_site(sx, sx, i, j) + two_site(sy, sy, i, j) + 0.5 * two_site(sz, sz, i, j)
        for i, j in chain.bonds)                           # XXZ ring, Delta = 0.5
assert close(H @ N_op, N_op @ H)                           # [H, N] = 0 ...
assert close(DU.from_commutant(DU.to_commutant(H)), H)     # ... so H is block diagonal (Proposition 4.10)
Hc = chain.xxz(J=1.0, Delta=0.5)                           # the same H, built sector by sector
E_sec = torch.cat([torch.linalg.eigvalsh(Hc.matrix[0, N, :k, :k]) for N, k in enumerate(chain.algebra.k_factors)])
assert close(torch.sort(E_sec).values, torch.linalg.eigvalsh(H))
```

## 4.5 $`SU(2)`$: spin

*Lecture notes:* [Section 4.5](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#s-4-5); [Theorem 4.11](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-11), [Proposition 4.13](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-13), [Theorem 4.14](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-14). Spin $`s`$ is `decompose.spin_matrices(s)`, and the total spin of $`L`$ spins $`\frac12`$ is `decompose.total_spin(L)`.

For $`L=4`$, [lecture notes, Proposition 4.13](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-13) gives $`\{S_x,S_y,S_z\}''\cong M_5\oplus M_3\oplus M_1`$
with multiplicities $`1,3,2`$, of dimension $`35`$, and the commutant $`\{S_x,S_y,S_z\}'\cong M_1\oplus M_3\oplus M_2`$, of
dimension $`14`$. The library finds the same from the three generators alone (sectors sorted by $`(k_c,m_c)`$), and
$`\mathbf S^2=S(S+1)`$ on spin $`S`$ ([Theorem 4.11](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-11)):

```python
S4 = dc.total_spin(4)
assert close(S4[0] @ S4[1] - S4[1] @ S4[0], 1j * S4[2])                # [S_x, S_y] = i S_z
D4 = dc.from_generators(S4)
print(D4)
assert D4.k_factors == (1, 3, 5) and D4.multiplicities == (2, 3, 1)     # M_1, M_3, M_5 with m = 2, 3, 1
assert D4.verify()['ok'] and D4.dims == (35, 14)
m_S = {S: math.comb(4, 2 - S) - (math.comb(4, 1 - S) if S < 2 else 0) for S in (0, 1, 2)}
assert [m_S[(k - 1) // 2] for k in D4.k_factors] == list(D4.multiplicities)    # Proposition 4.13
casimir = D4.to_algebra((S4 @ S4).sum(0))                                  # S^2 = S(S+1) on spin S
for c, k in enumerate(D4.k_factors):
    assert close(casimir.matrix[0, c, :k, :k], (k - 1) / 2 * (k + 1) / 2 * torch.eye(k))
```

By [lecture notes, Theorem 4.14](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-14) (Schur–Weyl duality) the site permutations span the commutant of the
total spin: $`\mathrm{span}\,\pi(S_L)=\{S_x,S_y,S_z\}'`$. For $`L\le5`$ the code compares the two spaces numerically: by
part (a) of the theorem, $`\mathrm{span}\,\pi(S_L)\subseteq\{S_x,S_y,S_z\}'`$, it suffices that they have the same dimension.

```python
for n in range(2, 6):
    comm = dc.commutant_basis(dc.total_spin(n)).reshape(-1, 4 ** n)          # a basis of {S_a}'
    perm = dc.permutation_representation(n, 2).reshape(-1, 4 ** n)          # all n! permutations
    dims = [torch.linalg.matrix_rank(A).item() for A in (comm, perm, torch.cat([comm, perm]))]
    print(n, dims)
    assert dims[0] == dims[1] == dims[2]                                     # span pi(S_n) = {S_a}'
Dp = dc.from_group(dc.permutation_representation(4, 2))                   # pi(S_4)'' = {S_a}'
print(Dp)
assert sorted(zip(Dp.multiplicities, Dp.k_factors)) == sorted(zip(D4.k_factors, D4.multiplicities))
```

The dimensions printed are $`2,5,14,42`$, the Catalan numbers $`\binom{2L}L/(L+1)`$; Corollary B.10 proves
$`\dim\{S_a\}'=\sum_Sm_S^2=\binom{2L}L/(L+1)`$ for every $`L`$. The last line is duality in
action: $`\pi(S_4)''`$ has blocks $`k=(1,2,3)`$ with multiplicities $`(5,1,3)`$ — the pairs $`(k_c,m_c)`$ of the total
spin with the roles exchanged. In general (Corollary B.9) the multiplicity space of spin $`S`$
carries an irreducible representation of $`S_L`$, of dimension $`m_S`$, and these are pairwise inequivalent; here the
dimensions are $`1,3,2`$ for $`S=2,1,0`$.

## 4.6 Spin systems

*Lecture notes:* [Section 4.6](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#s-4-6); [Proposition 4.15](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-15), [Theorem 4.16](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-16), [Corollary 4.17](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-17).

By [lecture notes, Corollary 4.17](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-17), orthonormal bases of the highest-weight vectors of $`L`$ spins
$`\frac12`$ can be built one site at a time with the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients of [Theorem 4.16](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-16).
Labelling each basis vector by its sequence of intermediate spins $`S_1=\frac12,S_2,\dots,S_L=S`$ gives the Yamanouchi
basis that `su2.SU2Chain` builds (its source uses exactly these coefficients; the code below checks only the
conclusions of Corollary 4.17), inside the $`U(1)`$ sector $`\hat N=\frac L2+S`$ of `SpinChain` (whose ordering of the
sites is a relabelling of the tensor factors, which commutes with the total spin by Lemma B.1(b)). The code checks
Theorem 4.16 against dense matrices and against the library's `su2.clebsch_gordan`, then the conclusions of
Corollary 4.17 for the bases of eight spins. We also check [Proposition 4.15(a)](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-15) on a ring of four spins
with `D4`, and compare the block sizes for eight spins.

```python
from torch_vn_algebra import su2

def ket(j, m):                                            # |j, m> of Theorem 4.11 (zero if |m| > j)
    v = torch.zeros(int(2 * j + 1), dtype=dt)
    if abs(m) <= j:
        v[int(j - m)] = 1
    return v

up, dn = ket(0.5, 0.5), ket(0.5, -0.5)
for j in (0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0):                            # Theorem 4.16
    Sj = dc.spin_matrices(j)
    sp_j = [math.sqrt(j * (j + 1) - (j - k) * (j - k + 1)) for k in range(1, int(2 * j) + 1)]
    assert close(Sj[0] + 1j * Sj[1], torch.diag(torch.tensor(sp_j, dtype=dt), 1))     # S_+ of Theorem 4.11
    assert close(Sj[2], torch.diag(torch.tensor([j - k for k in range(int(2 * j) + 1)], dtype=dt)))
    Sc = torch.stack([torch.kron(a, torch.eye(2, dtype=dt)) + torch.kron(torch.eye(len(a), dtype=dt), b)
                      for a, b in zip(Sj, dc.spin_matrices(0.5))])
    Sp, Sm = Sc[0] + 1j * Sc[1], Sc[0] - 1j * Sc[1]
    cols = []
    for J in (j + 0.5, j - 0.5):
        for M in [J - k for k in range(int(2 * J + 1))]:
            al, be = math.sqrt((j + M + 0.5) / (2 * j + 1)), math.sqrt((j - M + 0.5) / (2 * j + 1))
            p, q = (al, be) if J > j else (-be, al)
            x = p * torch.kron(ket(j, M - 0.5), up) + q * torch.kron(ket(j, M + 0.5), dn)
            assert close(Sc[2] @ x, M * x)
            if M == J:
                assert close(Sp @ x, 0)                               # highest weight
            else:
                assert close(Sm @ cols[-1], math.sqrt((J + M + 1) * (J - M)) * x)   # lowering, (c)
            for s2, e in ((0.5, up), (-0.5, dn)):                     # the library's Racah formula agrees
                cg = su2.clebsch_gordan(j, M - s2, 0.5, s2, J, M) if abs(M - s2) <= j else 0.0
                assert abs(cg - torch.vdot(torch.kron(ket(j, M - s2), e), x).real.item()) < 1e-12
            cols.append(x)
    U = torch.stack(cols, 1)
    assert U.shape == (2 * len(Sj[0]), 2 * len(Sj[0])) and close(U.mH @ U, torch.eye(U.shape[1]))   # (a)

def swap(i, j, n):
    p = list(range(n))
    p[i], p[j] = j, i
    return dc.permutation_representation(n, 2, [p])[0]

ring = [(i, (i + 1) % 4) for i in range(4)]
H4 = sum(swap(i, j, 4) / 2 - torch.eye(16, dtype=dt) / 4 for i, j in ring)        # Dirac's identity
assert close(H4, sum(two_site(a, a, i, j) for a in (sx, sy, sz) for i, j in ring))
h = D4.to_commutant(H4)                                   # blocks H_S of sizes m_S = 2, 3, 1
assert close(D4.from_commutant(h), H4)                    # H4 lies in {S_a}'
levels = torch.cat([torch.linalg.eigvalsh(h.matrix[0, c, :m, :m]).repeat_interleave(k)
                    for c, (k, m) in enumerate(zip(D4.k_factors, D4.multiplicities))])
assert close(torch.sort(levels).values, torch.linalg.eigvalsh(H4))   # level of H_S repeated 2S+1 times

u1, sym = SpinChain(8, boundary='periodic'), su2.SU2Chain(8, boundary='periodic')
for S, m in zip(sym.spins, sym.multiplicities):           # Corollary 4.17: orthonormal highest-weight bases
    B, N = sym.hw_basis(S), int(4 + S)                     # columns in the U(1) sector N = L/2 + S
    assert B.shape == (math.comb(8, N), m) and close(B.T @ B, torch.eye(m, dtype=B.dtype))
    assert N == 8 or close(sym.raise_(B, N), 0)            # S_+ B = 0 (S_z = S holds in sector N)
H_u1, H_su2 = u1.xxz(J=1.0, Delta=1.0), sym.heisenberg(J=1.0)
E_u1 = torch.sort(torch.cat([torch.linalg.eigvalsh(H_u1.matrix[0, N, :k, :k])
                             for N, k in enumerate(u1.algebra.k_factors)])).values
E_su2, S_lev, _ = sym.full_spectrum(H_su2, return_quantum_numbers=True)   # level of block S, 2S+1 times
E_su2, S_lev = E_su2[0], S_lev[0]
assert close(E_u1, E_su2)
assert S_lev[0] == 0 and (S_lev[1:4] == 1).all() and E_su2[4] - E_su2[3] > 1e-3     # singlet, then triplet
assert E_su2[1] - E_su2[0] > 1e-3 and close(E_su2[1:4], E_su2[1].expand(3))       # simple singlet (Theorem 9.9)
assert close(sym.restrict(H_u1).matrix, H_su2.matrix)      # Proposition 4.15(b)
flops = lambda ks: sum(k ** 3 for k in ks)
print(u1.algebra.k_factors, sym.multiplicities, flops(u1.algebra.k_factors), flops(sym.multiplicities))
print(f"ground state energy {E_su2[0].item():.6f}, first excited {E_su2[1].item():.6f} (x3)")
```

For the ring of eight spins the $`U(1)`$ blocks have sizes $`\binom8w=1,8,28,56,70,56,28,8,1`$ (Section 4.4), the $`SU(2)`$
blocks sizes $`m_S=14,28,20,7,1`$ for $`S=0,\dots,4`$ (Proposition 4.13). The cost of diagonalising, $`\sum k^3`$, drops from
$`739162`$ to $`33040`$, by a factor of $`22`$; the dimension of the algebra, $`\sum k^2`$, from
$`\sum_w\binom8w\binom8{8-w}=\binom{16}8=12870`$ (choose $`8`$ of $`16`$ elements, $`w`$ of them among the first $`8`$) to
$`\sum_Sm_S^2=1430=\binom{16}8/9`$ (Corollary B.10). By Theorem 9.9 (the ring has even length and $`J>0`$) the ground
state is a single singlet and $`E_0(0)<E_0(1)`$. *Numerical observation* (computed by the code above): the ground
energy is $`-3.651093`$, and the first excited level is a triplet at $`-3.128419`$.

## 4.7 Real algebras: real, complex and quaternionic type

*Lecture notes:* [Section 4.7](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#s-4-7). For a $`*`$-algebra $`\mathcal A`$ closed under complex conjugation, with a
decomposition $`V`$ whose column blocks $`V_c`$ belong to the sectors, [Theorem 4.20](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-20) gives the permutation
$`\sigma`$ of the sectors with $`\bar P_c=P_{\sigma(c)}`$, unitaries $`J_c`$, $`M_c`$ with $`\overline{V_c}=V_{\sigma(c)}(J_c\otimes M_c)`$
and, for $`\sigma(c)=c`$, the sign $`\varepsilon_c`$ with $`J_c\bar J_c=\varepsilon_c1`$; the types and the real algebra
$`\mathcal A_{\mathbb R}`$ are in [Theorem 4.21](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-21), the real Schur lemma is [Theorem 4.22](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-22), and the
real elements of the commutant are described by [Proposition 4.23](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#r-4-23).

`Decomposition.real_structure()` computes $`\sigma`$, $`J_c`$, $`M_c`$ and the types for a decomposition returned by
`wedderburn` (`from_generators`, `from_group`). It checks that the conjugates of the stored generators lie in
$`V(\bigoplus_cM_{k_c}\otimes1)V^*`$ (relative residual `'conjugation'`) and forms the unitary $`P=V^*\bar V`$, whose column block
$`c`$ is $`V^*\overline{V_c}`$: by Theorem 4.20(a) it is supported on the rows of the sector $`\sigma(c)`$, where it equals
$`J_c\otimes M_c`$. The library takes for $`\sigma(c)`$ the sector carrying the largest part of the block, requires
$`(k_{\sigma(c)},m_{\sigma(c)})=(k_c,m_c)`$ and bounds the rest (`'leak'`); it rearranges the block into the
$`k_c^2\times m_c^2`$ matrix with entries $`T_{(a,b),(j,l)}=T_{(a,j),(b,l)}`$, which for $`T=J\otimes M`$ is the rank-one matrix
$`\mathrm{vec}\,J\,(\mathrm{vec}\,M)^T`$, reads $`J_c`$ and $`M_c`$ off its leading singular vectors (with
$`\|J_c\|_F^2=k_c`$), and records the distance of the block from $`J_c\otimes M_c`$ (`'kron'`). If one of these three residuals
exceeds the tolerance, the shapes differ, or the partners do not form an involution, it raises `ValueError` (the
algebra is not closed under conjugation). For $`\sigma(c)=c`$ it sets $`\varepsilon_c`$ to the sign of $`\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Tr}(J_c\bar J_c)/k_c`$ —
exactly $`\pm1`$ in exact arithmetic, so the two candidates are $`2`$ apart — and records as
`'sign'` the maximum over these sectors of $`\|J_c\bar J_c-\varepsilon_c\|_F/\sqrt{k_c}`$ and
$`\|M_c\bar M_c-\varepsilon_c\|_F/\sqrt{m_c}`$, raised to at least $`1`$ if some $`\varepsilon_c=-1`$ with odd $`k_c`$ or $`m_c`$
(impossible by Theorem 4.20(b)). The result has `types`, `partner` ($`\sigma`$),
`J`, `M`, `residuals` and `ok`, which says that all residuals are at most the tolerance (by default the acceptance
threshold of the decomposition); then $`|\mathrm{Tr}(J_c\bar J_c)/k_c-\varepsilon_c|\le\|J_c\bar J_c-\varepsilon_c\|_F/\sqrt{k_c}`$
(Cauchy–Schwarz) is at most the tolerance too.

`Decomposition.real_basis()` returns a real orthogonal $`n\times n`$ matrix $`O`$ and a list that describes its blocks of columns (type, sectors,
column range): for a
sector of real type the columns $`O_c=V_c(W_J\otimes W_M)`$ of Theorem 4.21(a), so that $`O_c^TaO_c=\alpha_c(a)\otimes1_{m_c}`$
with real $`\alpha_c(a)`$; for a sector of quaternionic type the columns $`V_cW`$ with $`J_c\otimes M_c=WW^T`$ and
$`(J_c\otimes M_c)\bar W=W`$ (Lemma 4.19(c) with $`\varepsilon=1`$, as $`(J_c\otimes M_c)\overline{(J_c\otimes M_c)}=\varepsilon_c^2=1`$),
which are real by the computation of Theorem 4.21(a); and for a pair $`c,\sigma(c)`$ (with $`c<\sigma(c)`$) the columns $`\sqrt2\mathrm{Re}\,V_c`$ followed by
$`\sqrt2\mathrm{Im}\,V_c`$. These are orthonormal: $`V_c^*\overline{V_c}=V_c^*V_{\sigma(c)}(J_c\otimes M_c)=0`$ and
$`V_c^T\overline{V_c}=\overline{V_c^*V_c}=1`$, with their conjugates, give $`(\mathrm{Re}\,V_c)^T\mathrm{Re}\,V_c=(\mathrm{Im}\,V_c)^T\mathrm{Im}\,V_c=\frac12`$ and
$`(\mathrm{Re}\,V_c)^T\mathrm{Im}\,V_c=0`$; columns of different blocks lie in the ranges of the
orthogonal projections $`P_{\mathcal O}=\sum_{e\in\mathcal O}P_e`$ of different orbits $`\mathcal O`$ of $`\sigma`$ (as
$`\overline{V_c}=V_{\sigma(c)}(J_c\otimes M_c)`$), so $`O`$ is orthogonal. Taking real and imaginary parts of $`aV_c=V_cx`$, $`x=\pi_c(a)\otimes1_{m_c}`$,
for real $`a`$,
```math
O^TaO=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{Re}\,x&\mathrm{Im}\,x\\-\mathrm{Im}\,x&\mathrm{Re}\,x\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{on these columns.}
```
The Takagi factors $`W`$ with $`WW^T=J`$ of a symmetric unitary $`J=X+iY`$ are computed from an orthogonal
diagonalisation of $`X+tY`$ for a random $`t`$ ($`X`$ and $`Y`$ are commuting real symmetric matrices, since the imaginary part
of $`J\bar J=JJ^*=1`$ is $`YX-XY`$); the library accepts the result on its residuals $`\|WW^T-J\|_F/\|J\|_F`$ and
$`\|\mathrm{Im}(V_cW)\|_F/\sqrt{k_cm_c}`$ and warns (`DecompositionWarning`) if one of them exceeds
$`\max(\text{tolerance},10^3\,\epsilon_{\rm mach}\,n)`$.

[Section 4.7](../lecture_notes/04_symmetries.md#s-4-7) of the lecture notes works out three algebras generated by real matrices: (i) $`L_a\otimes1_2`$
on $`\mathbb R^3\otimes\mathbb R^2`$, with the generators $`(L_a)_{bc}=-\epsilon_{abc}`$ of the rotations of $`\mathbb R^3`$: one
sector of real type, $`k=3`$, $`m=2`$; (ii) $`\Omega_2\otimes1_2`$ on $`\mathbb R^2\otimes\mathbb R^2`$: a pair of complex type,
$`k=1`$, $`m=2`$; (iii) the left multiplications $`1_2\otimes L_q`$ by the quaternions on $`\mathbb H^2=\mathbb R^8`$: one sector of
quaternionic type, $`k=2`$, $`m=4`$. The code computes the decomposition and the real structure of each, and checks
Theorem 4.20(a),(b) and the block form of $`O^TaO`$ for the real type (Theorem 4.21(a)) and for the pair.

```python
def qmul(p, q):                                           # H = CD(C), Section E.6: (a, b)(c, d)
    (a1, b1), (a2, b2) = p, q
    return (a1 * a2 - b2.conjugate() * b1, b2 * a1 + b1 * a2.conjugate())

def q_left(q):                                            # left multiplication by q on H = R^4
    cols = []                                             # coordinates (Re a, Im a, Re b, Im b)
    for e in torch.eye(4, dtype=torch.float64).tolist():
        u, v = qmul(q, (complex(e[0], e[1]), complex(e[2], e[3])))
        cols.append([u.real, u.imag, v.real, v.imag])
    return torch.tensor(cols, dtype=torch.float64).T

L_so3 = torch.zeros(3, 3, 3, dtype=torch.float64)         # (L_a)_bc = -epsilon_abc
for i, (b1, b2) in enumerate([(1, 2), (2, 0), (0, 1)]):
    L_so3[i, b1, b2], L_so3[i, b2, b1] = -1.0, 1.0
R_rot = torch.tensor([[[0.0, -1.0], [1.0, 0.0]]])         # Omega_2

                 'C': (torch.kron(R_rot, eye2[None]), (1, 1), (2, 2)),        # Omega_2 (x) 1_2
                 'H': (torch.kron(eye2[None], L_H), (2,), (4,))}              # L_q on H^2 = R^8
dec_real = {}

    assert set(rs_t.types) == {t} and close(O_t.T @ O_t, torch.eye(D_t.n))     # real orthogonal basis
    for c, d in enumerate(rs_t.partner):                                      # Theorem 4.20
        o_c, o_d, w = D_t.offsets[c], D_t.offsets[d], ks[c] * ms[c]
        assert close(D_t.V[:, o_c:o_c + w].conj(), D_t.V[:, o_d:o_d + w] @ torch.kron(rs_t.J[c], rs_t.M[c]))
        if d == c:                                                            # J conj(J) = M conj(M) = +-1
            eps = 1.0 if t == 'R' else -1.0
            assert close(rs_t.J[c] @ rs_t.J[c].conj(), eps * torch.eye(ks[c]))
            assert close(rs_t.M[c] @ rs_t.M[c].conj(), eps * torch.eye(ms[c]))
    dec_real[t] = D_t, rs_t, O_t

D_t, rs_t, O_t = dec_real['R']
```

The code checks Theorem 4.22 on the irreducible real modules $`\mathbb R^3`$, $`\mathbb R^2`$, $`\mathbb R^4`$ of the three
examples (the types and multiplicities found by the library, real with $`m=1`$, a complex pair with $`m=1`$, quaternionic
with $`m=2`$, make them irreducible by Theorem 4.22(c)), computing the real
commutant as the null space of the real linear equations $`Xs=sX`$, $`Xs^T=s^TX`$, and then Proposition 4.23 for a random
real symmetric element of the commutant in each example.

```python
def real_commutant_dim(gens):                             # dim_R {X real : X s = s X, X s^T = s^T X}
    gens = torch.cat([gens, gens.mT])
    eye_n = torch.eye(gens.shape[-1], dtype=torch.float64)
    eqs = torch.cat([torch.kron(eye_n, s) - torch.kron(s.T.contiguous(), eye_n) for s in gens])
    return gens.shape[-1] ** 2 - torch.linalg.matrix_rank(eqs).item()

for gens, types, ms, dim_D in ((L_so3, ('R',), (1,), 1), (R_rot, ('C', 'C'), (1, 1), 2), (L_H, ('H',), (2,), 4)):
    D_irr = dc.from_generators(gens)                      # Theorem 4.22(c): R^3, R^2, R^4 are irreducible
    assert D_irr.real_structure().types == types and D_irr.multiplicities == ms
    assert real_commutant_dim(gens) == dim_D              # Theorem 4.22(b): D_U = R, C, H

gen_X = torch.Generator().manual_seed(1)
for t, (gens, ks, ms) in real_examples.items():           # Proposition 4.23
    D_t, rs_t, O_t = dec_real[t]
    B_t = dc.commutant_basis(gens)
    X_t = torch.einsum('r,rij->ij', torch.randn(len(B_t), dtype=dt, generator=gen_X), B_t).real
    X_t = X_t + X_t.T                                     # real symmetric, in A' (Lemma 4.18(b))
    assert close(X_t @ gens, gens @ X_t)
    x_t = D_t.to_commutant(X_t).matrix[0]
    ev = [torch.linalg.eigvalsh(x_t[c, :m, :m]) for c, m in enumerate(ms)]
    if t == 'R':                                          # (a): O^T X O = 1_3 (x) y, y real symmetric
        Y_t = O_t.T @ X_t @ O_t
        assert close(Y_t, torch.kron(torch.eye(3, dtype=torch.float64), Y_t[:2, :2]))
    if t == 'C':                                          # (c): the two sectors have the same spectrum
        assert close(ev[0], ev[1])
    if t == 'H':                                          # (b): Kramers pairs, each level 2 k_c = 4 times in X
        assert close(ev[0][0::2], ev[0][1::2])
        assert close(torch.linalg.eigvalsh(X_t), ev[0].repeat_interleave(2))
        print('quaternionic block x_c:', [round(v, 4) for v in ev[0].tolist()])
```

## 4.8 What to compare with

For finite groups, GAP and SageMath compute character tables exactly, and Corollary 4.5 then gives the
multiplicities; RepLAB (MATLAB; D. Rosset, F. Montealegre-Mora, J.-D. Bancal) decomposes representations
of finite and compact groups numerically, also with random elements of the commutant, and
SymbolicWedderburn.jl (Julia) computes Wedderburn decompositions for the symmetry reduction of
semidefinite programs. In physics, QuSpin and other exact-diagonalisation codes block-diagonalise
$`U(1)`$ and lattice symmetries, ITensor and TeNPy carry abelian charges in tensor networks, and
TensorKit.jl handles $`SU(2)`$ with fusion trees. The library's `decompose` is a dense method for small
matrices ($`n`$ up to a few hundred); it accepts any list of matrices (group elements or generators),
certifies its result (Proposition 4.9), and returns the algebra and its commutant as `TypeIAlgebra`
objects with `to_algebra`, `from_algebra`, `to_commutant`, `from_commutant`, so that Chapters 3, 5–8 apply
at once. For spin-$`\frac12`$ chains `su2.SU2Chain` gives the $`SU(2)`$ blocks directly (Corollary 4.17); it accepts up to
$`L=18`$ spins, a limit set by the memory of the bases.
